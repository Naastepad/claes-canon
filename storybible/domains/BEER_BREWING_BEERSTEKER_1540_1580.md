# Beer, brewing and biersteker practice — chapter-ready dossier

**Domain:** `WORLD.BEER_BREWING_DISTRIBUTION`  
**Status:** ACTIVE AUTHORING DOMAIN  
**Time window:** ca. 1540–1580 for core Claes/Cornelis use  
**Primary provenance:** `SRC-HIST-BEER-LOWCOUNTRIES-GOES-001`

## Governing canon

Cornelis is a **biersteker**: beer trade/distribution is part of his professional identity. He is **not** canonically the brewer and is not fixed as owner of the documented Nissepad brewery.

The brewery and the biersteker belong to the same material economy but are different roles. A chapter must ask which side of that chain it is actually showing.

## Production chain — background world

`malt grain → crush → mash/extract wort → boil with hops → cool → ferment → cask → store → transport`

This chain can supply sounds, smells, labour and material causality even when Cornelis does not perform the brewing steps himself.

### Production actors may include
- malt supplier/maltster;
- brewer/master and brewery workers;
- carriers;
- cooper/cask repair;
- biersteker/distributor;
- tapper/innkeeper/customer;
- civic tax/measure officials.

Do not collapse them into one generic 'brewer'.

## Cornelis-facing biersteker chain

1. **Order / negotiate** — quantity, condition, price, timing and source.
2. **Receive** — count casks, inspect seals/condition where appropriate, notice leakage or obvious spoilage.
3. **Record** — accounts, measures, debts, duties and customers.
4. **Store** — cellar/warehouse conditions, cask placement and losses matter.
5. **Move** — rolling/lifting/loading, cart/boat logistics, gates and streets.
6. **Deliver / redistribute** — tavern, household, institutional or other customer.
7. **Settle** — payment, credit, returns, shortages and disputes.

This gives Cornelis a believable commercial skillset without making him operate the mash tun every morning.

## Sensory grammar

For Cornelis, beer is often read as **condition + quantity + movement**:
- damp wood or stained floor → possible leakage/loss;
- smell turning sour/off → condition problem;
- cask weight/slosh → fullness and handling;
- cellar cool/damp versus street heat → storage risk;
- malt/wort/hops drifting from production space → proximity to brewery, not proof Cornelis is brewing.

For Claes, this is an early school in process: the same liquid changes with time; wood, air, heat and cleanliness matter; error can be smelled before it can be named.

## Beer vocabulary and categories

Use historically local terms only when sourced. Broad distinctions such as weaker/ordinary versus stronger/better beer are safer than a modern taxonomy.

Possible archival terms (`klein`, `spoel`, price-defined beer, `best/groot`, `hoppenbier`) require locality/date checking before hard prose. Never turn them into fixed modern styles.

## Hops / gruit rule

Do not write sixteenth-century Goes as if all beer is still generic medieval gruit ale. Hop brewing was long established in the Low Countries. A specific gruit practice needs local evidence.

## Nissepad brewery relationship

The documented Nissepad brewery with kettles, vats/casks and tools is an excellent material anchor for the wider family/business landscape, but:
- its 1577 documentation does not prove Cornelis owned it earlier;
- the Voorstad brewery reported burned in 1572 is not proven identical to the Nissepad brewery;
- family or business access can be fictionalized only under explicit story decisions.

## Scene uses

### Youth / father lesson
A task such as checking casks, counting deliveries or identifying loss can teach Claes that **attention precedes explanation**.

### Trade pressure
Beer connects rural grain, urban production, wood/casks, transport, taxation, credit and taverns. A shortage or damaged cask can dramatize network fragility without economic exposition.

### 1572 material rupture
If the novel later chooses to link military destruction to Claes' remaining Goese economic anchor, use the distinct brewery/property evidence carefully and keep the Nissepad/Voorstad identity open.

## Never write

- Cornelis casually described as `brouwer` merely because he is a biersteker;
- an exact Goese recipe or modern ABV/IBU;
- a mandatory gruit recipe;
- modern beer-style menus;
- the 1577 Nissepad brewery as proven Cornelis property;
- later brewery machinery projected backward without source support.
