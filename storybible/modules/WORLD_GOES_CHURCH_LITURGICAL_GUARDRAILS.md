# WORLD.GOES.CHURCH — liturgical and sensory guardrails

Status: ACTIVE COMPARATIVE WORLDBUILDING LAYER.

## Seating and movement
- Do not treat benches/chairs as post-Reformation inventions.
- Comparative late-medieval evidence shows seating was already common in urban and rural parish churches.
- Sitting, standing and walking are all historically possible.
- A bench in a 1540s scene is not inherently anachronistic.
- Exact Goese seating layout, ownership and social access remain locally unverified.

## Soundscape
- Do not write the church as a modern silent auditorium.
- Multiple liturgical/devotional activities, movement, speech, chant, low Masses and bells can overlap.
- Altar/sanctus bells function as attention-directing signals.
- Exact bell practice varies by location and diocese.

## Elevation
- High elevation of the consecrated host: strongly supported as a central late-medieval devotional moment.
- Chalice elevation: plausible in the period but not yet locally verified for Goes in this module.
- Do not canonize that host and chalice were certainly both elevated in Goes in 1544 without local/diocesan support.

## Silence rhythm
Preferred scene logic:
multiple sound fields -> bell/signal -> local concentration of attention -> looking/kneeling/temporary reduction of nearby noise -> liturgical moment -> ambient sound resumes.

Do not assume the whole building became absolutely silent.

## Outside bell
A sound signal audible outside around the central moment of Mass is comparatively plausible in western medieval practice, but a specific Goese klepkotje, rope, bell or protocol is LOCAL_UNVERIFIED.

## Sensory framework
Combine with `WORLD.RELIGIOUS_SPACE.SENSORY_CHURCH`:
body -> sinne -> spatial/social orientation -> comparison/pattern -> understanding -> moral interpretation.

Potential scene layers: cold stone, variable daylight, wax/incense/bodies/textiles/damp, Latin liturgical sound, vernacular speech/preaching, bells, organ, corporate signs, movement, standing, sitting, kneeling and crowding.
