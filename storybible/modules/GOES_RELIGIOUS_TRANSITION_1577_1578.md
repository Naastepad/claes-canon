# Goes — religious transition, 1577–1578

Primary source: `sources/SRC-GROENVELD-ETAL-TACHTIGJARIGE-OORLOG-2008.md`.
Claims: `claims/SOURCE_CLAIMS_GOES_RELIGION_1577_1578.yaml`.

## Historical anchors
- March 1577: Goes, the last royalist town in Zeeland, concluded a satisfactie with William of Orange. Catholicism remained the only permitted religion under that settlement.
- 1578: during radicalisation, Calvinist preaching was introduced under pressure in the Holland and Zeeland towns that had concluded satisfacties. This applies to Goes as a satisfactie town.
- July 1578: Orange proposed a religious peace with reciprocal worship rights; the proposal met resistance.
- January 1574: rebel forces defeated a Habsburg/Spanish fleet near Reimerswaal, contributing to Middelburg's surrender the following month.

## Storybible use
For Goes, use the sequence: Catholic public continuity in March 1577 → growing confessional pressure → Reformed preaching during 1578 → contested control of public religious space. Do not equate this with an immediate population-wide conversion.

Do not invent an exact 1578 date, first Goese church used for Reformed preaching, named local activists, or a specific sequence of altar/image removal without local evidence. The Reimerswaal 1574 anchor concerns a naval battle near the town, not proven street fighting inside it.
