# Mayken Adriaensdr. Lampert — geliefde / apothekersdochter

**Status:** CANON for the fictional character; historical anchors remain explicitly qualified.

## Canonical identity

**Mayken Adriaensdr. Lampert**, born circa **1546 in Goes**, is Claes' beloved and later partner in the material/botanical verification line. In everyday prose she is normally called **Mayken**. In a deed-like register she may appear as **Mayken Adriaens** or **Mayken Adriaensdochter**.

She is a **fictional daughter** placed in the historically attested Goese apothecary environment around **Adriaen Jacopsen / Adriaen Jacopsen Lampert**. The current source corpus does **not** document a daughter of Adriaen; her existence and kinship are novel canon.

## Historical frame and certainty

### Documented
- A **Jacob/Jacop Lampart/Lambert, apothecary** is attested in Goes in the 1530s-1540s.
- In 1539 he appears with **Merricken his wife**.
- In 1545 **Merricken, wife of Jacob Lambert apothecary**, acquires property at the Oprel.
- A **Mayken, wife of Jacop Lampart**, sells a house on the Molendijk in 1543. This proves that *Mayken* is a historical female name inside the Lampart milieu, but does not prove that Mayken and Merricken are the same woman.
- An **Adriaen Jacopsen apteker** is attested in Goes in 1554 and 1556.
- **Adriaen Jacopsen Lampert** is repeatedly attested in the Goese property records in the 1550s.
- Property continuity, including **Den Eenhoorn**, strongly links the older Lampart/Lambert apothecary environment to Adriaen Jacopsen Lampert.
- Adriaen's house **De/Nieuwe Zwaene** on the north side of the Grote Markt / Oprel is recorded after the 1554 fire as a **verbrand huis**.

### Supported / probable, not proved
- `Adriaen Jacopsen apteker` and `Adriaen Jacopsen Lampert` are treated as the same historical man for story modelling only where the context requires it; the identification is highly plausible but not yet deed-explicit.
- Adriaen is plausibly the son or successor of the older Jacob Lambert/Lampart apothecary, but no currently available deed explicitly states that father-son relation.

### Fiction / canon
- Mayken is Adriaen's daughter.
- She is born circa 1546.
- She becomes Claes' beloved and partner.
- Her mother remains unspecified until separately decided.

## Naming rationale

The name **Mayken** is deliberately selected because a `Mayken huisvrouw Jacop Lampart` is actually attested in the 1543 Goese transport corpus. This is a historical naming anchor, not proof of descent or namesake logic. Do not assert that fictional Mayken was named after that historical woman unless a later decision explicitly establishes it.

## Age line

Working age model:
- 18 May 1554 fire: about 7-8;
- 1561: about 14-15;
- 1566: about 19-20;
- 1570 recovery work: about 23-24;
- 1572 siege: about 25-26;
- 1585: about 38-39.

This places her roughly three to four years younger than Claes. They may know of each other in youth, but the canon does not require a childhood romance.

## Dramatic function

Mayken is not a convenience character who merely supplies Dodoens. Her own competence grows from an apothecary household: materials, simples, smell, taste, texture, drying, preparation, lists, comparison and practical error checking. She is strong where Claes is strongest only after he has transformed: embodied discrimination joined to action.

She is indispensable to the 1570 recovery sequence in **materials, botany, Dodoens loci, error control and contradiction**. Claes remains indispensable to the personal merels/Monas memory chain and final responsibility. Neither can complete the whole recovery architecture alone.

The 1554 fire gives them a non-identical shared historical environment. Claes loses mother, brother, unborn sibling and home. Mayken's historical family model loses at least the Zwaene property. Her fictional household need not suffer the same deaths. The preferred dramatic asymmetry is that she knows fire and rebuilding without reproducing Claes' exact trauma.

In the later road-to-Enkhuizen line she is a companion and catalyst for Claes' recovery of the *sinne*, never the agent who heals him for him.

## Guardrails

- Never present Mayken as a historically documented daughter of Adriaen Lampert.
- Never claim the current transport corpus proves Adriaen's complete household.
- Preserve the distinction between documented Jacob/Jacop Lampart/Lambert, likely family/professional continuity, and fictional kinship.
- The Weeskamer/testament route remains the best archival avenue for possible future discovery of real children; any new primary evidence may refine the historical model without automatically replacing fictional Mayken.
- `Lampert` is used as the story family/bynname; deed-like naming may use the patronymic `Adriaens(dr.)`.
