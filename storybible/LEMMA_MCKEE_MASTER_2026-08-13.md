## 1. Governing proposition

**Controlling idea — `THEME.CLAES.CONTROLLING_IDEA`**  
A man who spends his life seeking hidden order becomes whole only when knowledge ceases to be a means of control and becomes responsibility toward other people.

**Dramatic question — `THEME.CLAES.DRAMATIC_QUESTION`**  
What does truth ask of Claes toward the other when certainty, safety and control are impossible?

**Character engine**
- Desire: understand the hidden order behind visible reality.
- Psychological need: recover trust in embodied perception — the *sinne* — and act without complete certainty.
- Moral need: understand that knowledge and perception increase responsibility toward the other.
- Gift: prolonged, exact, embodied observation.
- Shadow: continuing to observe after action is required.
- Child lie: if I look carefully enough, I can prevent surprise or loss.
- Adult lie: if I understand the process, I can control the consequences.
- Final truth: right action does not require certainty of success; mature mastery can transmit and release rather than possess.

Canonical anchors: `STC.CLAES.SINNE.001`, `STC.CLAES.PARADOX.001`, `STC.CLAES.NEED.001`, `STC.CLAES.MORAL_QUESTION.001` and the decisions of 13 August 2026.

## 4. Claes — cradle to grave and the *sinne*

### I — 1542–ca. 1547: body before meaning
The world enters Claes through warmth, cold, smell, light, sound, silence, rhythm, food, wax, incense and touch before language. The body knows before the mind explains.

### II — ca. 1547–1554: discovering order
Home, church, school, brewing, trade and merels teach relation and process. The *sinne* develops through sensation → recognition → comparison → pattern → understanding. Cornelis trains attention through waiting, smelling, counting and looking again.

### III — 1554–1561: first blackening
The Goes fire, displacement to Reimerswaal, water threat and later losses change open perception into vigilance. Claes learns that seeing accurately does not prevent change or loss. The wound begins: *I did not see early enough what was happening.*

### IV — 1560–1564: disciplined interpretation
As older pupil, then in Antwerp, perception becomes analytic and practical. Landjuweel, print culture, copying, commerce, Silvius and Dee teach that carrier, role, intention and effect can diverge. Claes moves from observer to causal actor.

### V — 4 Oct 1564–1578: agency under consequence
Secrecy, compartmentalisation, Cornelis' fall and the 1570 recovery architecture intensify the adult lie that process can be controlled. The code is solved only through weeks of work and collaboration; knowledge becomes moral burden rather than private possession.

### VI — 1578–1584: sensory and moral constriction
Accumulated trauma, secrecy, consequence and responsibility blunt or constrict the *sinne*. The wound changes into: *I saw and still did not do enough.* Delft 1584 is the moral bottom.

### VII — 1584–1602+: recovery, resonance, sovereignty
On the road toward Enkhuizen, with his beloved beside him — the apothecary's daughter whose identity and full biography remain open — Claes rediscovers embodied perception. She accompanies and catalyses the process but does not perform it for him. Sensation begins to echo and resonate within the self rather than merely registering danger outside it.

The recovered *sinne* is not a return to childhood innocence. It becomes a deeper sequence:

**perceive → distinguish → choose → carry → release**

This is simultaneously trauma recovery, moral maturation and the lived Great Work. Claes grows toward wisdom and inner sovereignty as an alchemist not because he escapes matter, but because he learns to read and transform his relation to matter, body, memory and the other.

### Memory architecture — Pollmann guardrail
Claes' memory must not behave like a modern internal archive. Early modern memory is socially and materially mediated: event, personal recollection, repeated story, ritual, song, image, object, place and public narrative can reinforce, alter or contest one another. Fire, flood, war, church interiors, books, tools, melodies and routes can therefore become memory carriers without being reduced to symbols.

Violence need not immediately produce speech. Silence, delayed narration, shame, guilt, pain and continuing danger are historically plausible pressures around memories of violence. This supports Claes' sensory constriction after accumulated losses, but it does **not** diagnose him retrospectively with a modern disorder. Recovery likewise need not mean accurate retrieval of a fixed past: it can mean learning to encounter carriers of memory without allowing them to dictate perception and action.

Source: `sources/SRC-POLLMANN-MEMORY-EARLY-MODERN-EUROPE-2017.md`.

## 5. Spiritual journey — matter as vessel

`DEC.CLAES.SPIRITUAL_JOURNEY.2026-08-13`

Claes' life question moves from **“What is true?”** to **“What does this truth ask of me toward the other?”** This is his movement from matter toward spirituality **through matter, not away from it**.

Senses, bodies, books, paper, ink, plants, bread, fire, water, metals, salts, craft, music and alchemical operations are vessels of discernment. The Great Work is the transmutation:

**knowledge-as-control → discernment → responsibility → wisdom-in-relation → transmission-and-release.**

## 11. World modules and historical discipline

Goes, Reimerswaal, Antwerp, Delft, Middelburg and Enkhuizen remain source-weighted historical environments. Distinguish historical anchor, reconstruction and fiction. Do not manufacture exact topography, school personnel, institutional structures, trade practices or religious affiliations where sources do not support them.

### Sensory religious space — `WORLD.RELIGIOUS_SPACE.SENSORY_CHURCH`
The active scene/world module `narrative/religious_space_sensory_church.yaml`, grounded primarily in Wendy Wauters (`sources/SRC-WAUTERS-RELIGIOUS-SPACE-2021.md`), governs reconstruction of pre-Tridentine church experience where relevant. It treats the church simultaneously as liturgical institution, sensory field, social map, corporate stage and memory repository. The documented Antwerp model may be used directly for Antwerp; broader Low Countries sensory and corporative mechanisms may inform Goes and Reimerswaal only with scale correction and explicit local verification for numbers, dedications, ownership, topography, feast customs, processional routes and post-1566 chronology.

For Claes, this module provides historical scaffolding for the *sinne* sequence: **body → sinne → spatial/social orientation → comparison/pattern → understanding → moral discernment**. It also defines scene guardrails for guilds, crafts, confraternities, rhetoricians, bells, processions, light, smell, crowd movement, graves and the church as memory palace. Use it as historical scene infrastructure, not as proof that every local feature documented in Antwerp existed identically in Goes or Reimerswaal.

### Rederijkers as civic-religious network
Van Bruaene's synthesis (`sources/SRC-VAN-BRUAENE-OM-BETERS-WILLE-2008.md`) requires the rederijkers to be treated as more than literary decoration. A chamber is a civic corporation and communication network in which drama, lyric, competition, craft and trade sociability, urban honour, lay devotion and religious argument can meet.

For Claes this gives the **1561 Antwerp Landjuweel** structural weight. It is a public school in signs: blazons, costumes, processions, rhetoric, prizes, rival chambers, languages, professions, patronage and contested religious meanings teach him that the same carrier can communicate differently to different communities. The rederijker milieu can therefore prepare the later code architecture without itself becoming a cipher school.

Van Bruaene also supports a dynamic rather than binary confessional field: rederijkers could participate in civic religion while creative and critical textual practice contributed to more interiorized, intellectualized and sometimes reform-minded religious discourse. This makes the chamber a plausible hinge between Claes' inherited Catholic sensory world and the increasingly contested interpretive world of the 1560s.

Guardrail: South-Netherlandish patterns and Antwerp evidence must not be silently copied into Goes or Reimerswaal. Local chamber membership, offices, ritual obligations, exact competitions and confessional positions require local evidence.

### Catholic Scripture, liturgy and confessional change, ca. 1550–1580
For Claes' formative world, keep **liturgical Scripture** and **vernacular Bible ownership/reading** separate.

- In Catholic practice in the mid-sixteenth-century Low Countries, **Latin is the official language of the Church** and the **Vulgate** is the authoritative Latin biblical text (`SC.HIST.CATHOLIC_BIBLE.LATIN_VULGATE.1550.001`). A scene in which Claes hears Scripture in Mass should therefore default to Latin proclamation/chant and vernacular explanation or preaching rather than a Dutch Bible being read as the normative liturgical text.
- The **Leuven Bible of 1548**, Nicolaas van Winghe's approved Catholic Dutch translation based on the Vulgate, is historically appropriate as a Catholic vernacular reading, devotional, educational or clerical aid (`SC.HIST.LEUVEN_BIBLE.1548.001`). The digital edition confirms a 1553 revision and a Plantin Antwerp reprint with verse division in 1565–1566. Its presence in a household or study is plausible but must not be assumed for a specific character without narrative or evidentiary support.
- The **Liesvelt Bible** is materially and confessionally different: its New Testament follows Luther and the last Liesvelt edition appeared in 1542 (`SC.HIST.LIESVELT_BIBLE.1526_1542.001`). If a Liesvelt Bible reaches Goes later in the novel, treat that object as potentially charged by reformational provenance, risk, memory or identity rather than as the ordinary Catholic Bible.
- **Lucas 8:4–15, the parable of the sower**, belongs to all these textual traditions. For an early Catholic memory in Claes, the strongest historically grounded anchor is the Vulgate formulation and its liturgical/preaching reception; a vernacular wording should be tied to an actual Dutch reading context rather than silently substituted for the Mass.
- The Revolt transforms Catholic identity and practice over time rather than producing an instantaneous uniform switch (`SC.HIST.CATHOLIC_IDENTITY.REVOLT.1520_1635.001`). Exact local implementation remains source-dependent; do **not** generalize a Holland/England model onto Goes without local evidence.

Narrative use: Catholic ritual can function as an embodied *sinne* environment — sound, incense, wax, gesture, kneeling, elevation, silence, sung Latin, architecture and calendrical repetition — but this must remain concrete historical experience first. Any alchemical resonance (matter, transformation, patience, hidden/manifest meaning) is secondary narrative interpretation, not a claim that Catholic liturgy was historically understood as alchemy.

### Goes religious transition — 1577–1578
The local chronology is now partly source-anchored through Groenveld, Leeuwenberg, Mout and Zappey, *De Tachtigjarige Oorlog* (`sources/SRC-GROENVELD-ETAL-TACHTIGJARIGE-OORLOG-2008.md`; detailed module `storybible/modules/GOES_RELIGIOUS_TRANSITION_1577_1578.md`; claims `claims/SOURCE_CLAIMS_GOES_RELIGION_1577_1578.yaml`).

- **March 1577:** Goes, the last royalist town in Zeeland, reaches a satisfactie with William of Orange. Under that settlement Catholicism remains the only permitted religion.
- **1578:** in the subsequent radicalisation, Calvinist/Reformed preaching is introduced under pressure in the Holland and Zeeland towns that had concluded satisfacties. Goes belongs to this class; therefore the Catholic-only March 1577 settlement must not be projected unchanged through 1578.
- **July 1578:** Orange's proposal for a religious peace shows that coexistence, confessional monopoly and public worship remain actively contested rather than resolved by a single clean transition.

Scene rule: distinguish **public worship arrangements** from **personal confession**. The shift is best dramatized through changing speakers, permitted rituals, access, crowd behaviour, magistracy and control of church space, not through an implausible instant conversion of the population. Exact 1578 date, first Goese church used for Reformed preaching, named local activists/preachers and altar/image-removal sequence remain open pending local evidence.

### Reimerswaal 1574 — Delta war anchor
The same source places a rebel naval victory over a Habsburg/Spanish fleet **near Reimerswaal in January 1574**; the defeat cut maritime relief to Middelburg, which surrendered the following month. Use this as a strong waterway-war backdrop for the Reimerswaal/Delta world. Do not turn 'near Reimerswaal' into unsupported street fighting inside the town.

### Revolt, violence and public memory
Pollmann's memory synthesis provides a guardrail for the later political world. The Revolt generates not one stable shared account but competing memories carried by stories, sermons, songs, prints, objects, rituals and places. Public memory can shape personal recollection, while individual stakeholders can also reshape public memory.

For scenes after 1566, distinguish four layers: **what happened → what Claes perceived → what is subsequently told → what a community chooses to remember or forget**. They need not coincide. This is especially important around iconoclasm, sieges, violence, Cornelis' fate, the 1578 public consequence and Delft 1584.

Do not use later atrocity narratives as transparent eyewitness evidence for an earlier scene. They can instead appear as evidence of how violence is narrated, moralized, politicized and transmitted. Likewise, political acts of forgetting do not erase embodied or private memory.

Huis der Liefde/Radermacher material may function as network or late spiritual/social mirror but never as unsupported proof of formal Familist membership or proto-Masonic institutional continuity.
