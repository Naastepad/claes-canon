# Goes clergy — Mathijs Jacopsen/Jacobsen and Clemens van den Dale, 1541–1564

**Status:** CANON historical/world dossier.  
**Decision anchors:** `DEC.GOES.CLERGY.MATHIJS_VICE_CUREYT.2026-08-16`, `DEC.GOES.CLERGY.CLEMENS_VAN_DEN_DALE.2026-08-16`, `DEC.GOES.CLERGY.SUCCESSION_BOUNDARY.2026-08-16`.  
**Primary provenance:** `SRC-HIST-GOES-CLERGY-RAZE-1536-1564-001`.

## Canonical conclusion

The named historical priestly anchor for the Catholic Goese world immediately before and at the beginning of Claes' birth year is **mr. Mathijs Jacopsen/Jacobsen**.

He is not merely inferred from a generic title. The Goese transport records repeatedly name him as a cleric and progressively sharpen the office:

- 21 March 1536: `mr. Mathijs Jacopsen pastoor`;
- 14 March 1541: `mr. Mathijs Jacopsen, vice-pastoor`;
- 27 June 1541: `Mathijs Jacopsen pastoor`, buying a house `aan het kerkhof`;
- 23 January 1542: `Mathijs Jacopsen pastoor`, selling a house `achter het koor van de kerk`;
- **27 February 1542: `mr. Mathijs Jacobsen vice-cureyt ter Goes`.**

The last wording is the decisive local-office anchor. It establishes Mathijs as a deputy cure-of-souls / parochial priest functioning **in Goes** at that date.

For the novel, Mathijs may therefore replace an invented anonymous priest in any scene whose chronology actually falls within the evidence-bounded period. He is especially useful as the human face of the Catholic institutional world Claes is born into.

## Critical date boundary: Claes' birth

Claes' canonical birth date is **8 December 1542**.

The evidence currently proves Mathijs in the Goese vice-cureit office on **27 February 1542**, not on 8 December. Therefore the canon must distinguish:

- **safe:** Mathijs is a documented Goese vice-cureit in Claes' birth year and a historical clergy anchor for the world immediately preceding Claes' birth;
- **not safe:** Mathijs was certainly still vice-cureit on 8 December 1542;
- **not safe:** Mathijs baptized Claes;
- **not safe:** Mathijs personally attended the Nissepat household at Claes' birth.

If the exact celebrant or baptizing priest becomes narratively necessary, further benefice/collation or parish evidence is required.

## Maria Magdalena connection

The project associates Mathijs' Goese cure with the **Maria Magdalenaparochie / Grote Kerk environment** because:

1. his office is explicitly `vice-cureyt ter Goes`;
2. his property transactions place him repeatedly at the kerkhof and behind the choir of the church;
3. the Maria Magdalena church is the central Goese parochial environment relevant to this period.

This is a **SUPPORTED contextual identification**, not a verbatim reading of the 27 February act. That act says `ter Goes`; it does not itself write `Maria Magdalena`.

## What `vice-cureit` means for story use

The distinction between titular benefice-holder and local cure-of-souls matters. Mathijs need not be treated as the ultimate holder of the benefice. The evidence supports the more concrete and narratively useful role: a priest actually functioning in the local parochial system.

This permits scenes in which Mathijs is encountered in the ordinary Catholic life of Goes — church, kerkhof, liturgical or pastoral surroundings — while avoiding invented claims about the unseen higher benefice structure.

## Spatial embedding

Mathijs is unusually useful because the transport records attach him to real urban space:

- kerkhof;
- behind the choir of the church;
- Langevorststraat;
- Zuideinde;
- Handbogenhof environment;
- a `pastoors capelrie` / `capelrie mr. Mathijs pastoor` appears in the same property network.

These anchors make him part of the living city rather than an abstract office-holder.

**Spatial guardrail:** ownership, purchase, sale and adjacency are not residence. Do not infer a parsonage or sleeping place unless separately documented.

## Later clergy anchor: Clemens van den Dale

A second strong anchor appears on **20 March 1564**:

**Clemens van den Dale, `licentiaat pastoor Goes`**, buys the house *de Lombaert* on the north side of the Grote Markt.

This title is direct and specific. Clemens therefore belongs to the verified institutional world of Goes in 1564.

His presence does **not** establish that Claes knows or meets him. In early 1564 Claes' main canonical trajectory lies in the Antwerp/Dee/Silvius formation; Clemens is first of all a world-state anchor for Goes.

## The unresolved middle: 1542–1563

The current recovered transport evidence does not provide a complete succession list between Mathijs and Clemens.

Remain unresolved:

- who held the titular Goese pastorate above or alongside Mathijs in 1541–1542;
- how long Mathijs remained vice-cureit after 27 February 1542;
- who served during the later 1540s and 1550s;
- whether Clemens entered office shortly before 1564 or had already served earlier.

Do not repair this gap by extending either man's tenure by plausibility alone.

## Scene-use rule

For Claes' childhood church material:

- a scene in **1541 or early 1542** may safely name Mathijs in his documented Goese clerical role;
- a scene on **8 December 1542** must leave the exact priest unnamed unless further evidence is found;
- later childhood scenes must not continue Mathijs automatically;
- a **1564 Goes** institutional reference may name Clemens van den Dale, but only create a Claes–Clemens interaction if separately chosen and chronologically plausible.

This dossier governs together with `WORLD.GOES.CLERGY_1541_1564` and the existing Grote-Kerk/sensory-church material.
