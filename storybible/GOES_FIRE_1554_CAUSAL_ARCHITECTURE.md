This module fixes the **fictional causal architecture** around the historical Goese fire without converting documentary gaps, later analogies or authorial choices into archival facts. It governs the transition from Claes' intact Goese childhood to the Reimerswaal period and supplies the durable backline for Cornelis, Tanneken, Mayken and the Nissepat family sign.

---

## 1. Historical core already fixed in the repository

The current source-weighted fire reconstruction remains authoritative:

- the catastrophic fire occurs on **18 May 1554**;
- the Goese salt-work world lies around the northern harbour-industrial edges, including Oostzelke and Westzelke;
- the current source-weighted origin is **Oostzelke**;
- a strong north-easterly wind drives destructive spread broadly west/south-west;
- the exact minute-by-minute ignition sequence is not preserved;
- the historical casualty count remains **UNKNOWN** in the current evidence bundle;
- the specific destruction of the Nissepat Nieuwstraat household and the named family deaths are **novel canon**, not recovered victim evidence.

The current fire model may use wind-driven secondary ignitions / a multi-front `brandwaaier` qualitatively. It must not invent archival clock times, exact ember distances or a modern technical fire report.

### Historical/fiction boundary

The following must never be collapsed into one claim:

1. **historical fire origin zone** — source-backed;
2. **the exact individual keet/parcel** — not yet fixed historically;
3. **Cornelis' connection to an implicated keet** — fiction canon;
4. **the technical ignition mechanism** — open;
5. **who committed an operational error, if anyone** — open;
6. **public attribution to Cornelis** — fiction canon;
7. **legal or moral guilt** — deliberately unresolved.

## 2. The salt world Claes inhabits

The fire must emerge from an ordinary economic system rather than from a plot-only accident.

For the novel's world, the Goese zoutkeet is understood as a harbour-linked, fire-dependent salt-refining workplace. The current historical research direction strongly supports a sixteenth-century Zeeland transition in which older local `darink/zel` extraction exists alongside, and increasingly gives way to, refinement of imported rough/baaizout. For **Cornelis' fictional keet**, the authorial working model is therefore:

**incoming rough salt / brine-related feedstock -> heated refinement in large pans -> drying/condition control -> storage -> onward trade**

This process model is a Storybible reconstruction aid, not permission to import every later documented zoutziederij detail into 1554 Goes.

### Material scene vocabulary that is safe in principle

- a predominantly wooden or otherwise highly combustible industrial structure;
- large heated salt pans;
- sustained fire and fuel;
- heat, steam/damp, salt residue, smoke and smell;
- sacks, barrels/containers, loading and unloading;
- harbour transport and workers;
- separation between economic interest and daily operation.

### Research guardrail

Terms or practices such as a specific `denne`, `keetspek`, exact clarification additives, exact pan dimensions, exact turf-handling rules, exact worker hierarchy and exact clothing require period- and place-specific confirmation before hard prose. Later Alkmaar/Goese/Arnemuiden examples may illuminate structural possibilities but do not automatically document Goes 1554.

## 3. Cornelis: circulation rather than manufacture

Cornelis' beer, salt and text worlds are now governed by one economic character principle:

> **Cornelis does not primarily make the product; he makes circulation possible.**

He is a Goese poorter and biersteker/beer trader. His competence lies in:

- property interests and access;
- credit and accounts;
- storage and condition checking;
- barrels and harbour logistics;
- routes and timing;
- judging goods and people;
- delegating specialist work;
- connecting producers, carriers, buyers and trusted contacts;
- later, moving papers/texts through networks he already knows from trade.

This does **not** make him a passive rentier. His work is judgement, organization, transmission and responsibility.

### Salt interest

In fiction canon Cornelis has a financial/economic ownership interest in one Goese salt-refining keet. He is **not** its daily zoutzieder and does not personally control every firing, pan or work shift. An operational lead and workers conduct the daily technical process.

The exact form remains open: sole ownership, partnership, share, lease interest or another historically plausible arrangement has not yet been fixed.

### Strength and shadow

Cornelis' strength is scalable trust:

**choose -> arrange -> delegate -> check -> route**

Its shadow is incomplete control:

**own / finance / organize -> depend on others -> remain responsible when the system fails**

The fire therefore tests a core assumption without needing to make Cornelis personally careless with a flame:

> **connection and ownership are not the same as control.**

Keep that meaning author-side; scenes should show consequences rather than state the thesis.

## 4. Tanneken: Body / Continuity

Tanneken remains the primary source of Claes' early embodied sensory knowledge, but her function is broader than domestic atmosphere.

Her competence is **trained sensation in the continuity of a working household and its attached economy**. Through repetition she knows:

- what is actually present rather than merely recorded or promised;
- whether food, cloth, grain, drink or other material is in good condition;
- what is too damp, too dry, spoiled, short, late or wasteful;
- ordinary consumption and shortage;
- who is reliable, ill, absent, exhausted or in need;
- what a commercial decision means once it reaches bodies, fuel, time and daily work.

The durable complement is:

> **Cornelis knows circulation; Tanneken knows condition.**

Their marriage therefore has an economic as well as emotional complementarity. Tanneken need not hold a formal office in Cornelis' keet to know workers, notice discrepancies or understand practical consequences.

### Hard limit

Do not turn Tanneken into a retrospective polymath. She is not automatically:

- zoutzieder;
- keetmeesteres;
- professional bookkeeper;
- apothecary;
- formal business manager;
- infallible safety inspector.

Her competence remains local, repeated, fallible and practical.

### Consequence of her death

When Tanneken dies, Cornelis loses more than spouse and co-parent. He also loses part of the **operational memory and continuity** that made the household/economic system work reliably. This helps explain why post-fire recovery is harder even though Cornelis remains intelligent and well connected.

This distinction also protects Mayken's later role:

- **Tanneken:** lived operational/material knowledge;
- **Mayken:** later specialized material identification, preparation, testing and contradiction.

## 6. Claes: the first catastrophic failure of attention

Before the fire Claes' intelligence often succeeds through:

**sensation -> recognition -> comparison -> pattern -> understanding**

On 18 May the world changes faster than that sequence can complete. He cannot see every fire front, conversation, movement or death. He receives the event as fragments: smoke, alarm, conflicting reports, blocked routes, waiting, absence and later confirmation.

Claes is not safely elsewhere. He is eleven and remains inside the Goese fire event, experiencing alarm, escape, separation, waiting and searching directly, while surviving away from the family house. Cornelis is absent from the immediate fire zone during the decisive household-loss interval. Their later grief therefore begins from unequal knowledge: neither witnessed what the other did, and neither can supply the other's missing experience.

This creates a formative false possibility:

> **If I had seen earlier or better, perhaps loss could have been prevented.**

Claes is **not factually responsible** for Tanneken, Jan or the unborn child. The wound is epistemic and relational, not causal.

The fire therefore helps create the later danger already present in his character:

**attention -> understanding -> imagined control**

His mature work is eventually to learn:

**perceive -> distinguish -> choose -> carry -> release**

### Important contradiction

The fire must not rewrite Claes into an inherently passive child. `De Kraan` remains evidence that when no deliberative interval exists, Claes can act immediately. His danger appears especially when uncertainty gives him time to keep observing and reconstructing.

## 7. Catholic public aftermath and Cornelis' religious dissonance

Goes in 1554 remains publicly Catholic. Communal interpretation of the catastrophe therefore belongs first inside a Catholic civic-religious world rather than a modern secular disaster frame.

Potential historically appropriate categories to research for scene use include:

- Mass/prayer;
- burial and mourning;
- charity/almsgiving;
- penance;
- providence and judgement;
- mercy;
- communal obligation after catastrophe.

The **exact local rite, sermon wording, procession or institutional response remains OPEN until sourced**.

### Dramatic guardrail

Do not make a priest a convenient prosecutor who publicly names Cornelis without evidence. A stronger model is:

**general religious language about sin, judgement, transience, penance or mercy -> local listeners apply it to an already circulating rumour.**

Thus a sermon can remain general while townspeople hear a specific accusation into it.

### Cornelis

Cornelis' Huis der Liefde/Familist affiliation already exists by approximately 1552–1553 in novel canon. Therefore:

- the fire **does not convert him**;
- he remains outwardly embedded in Catholic civic life;
- Claes can notice that his father participates yet resists a simple equation between catastrophe and a named divine punishment;
- Claes does not possess the theological vocabulary to diagnose the difference.

The fire is a **test of an existing inward commitment**, not the origin of it.

## 8. Post-fire social and economic erosion

The family catastrophe is followed by a slower civic/economic one.

Possible consequences of the fire and its attribution include:

- destroyed or damaged household capital;
- damage to the salt interest and/or stock;
- disruption of workers and transport;
- claims, debts and repair costs;
- increased caution from trading partners;
- weakening credit;
- reputational damage because Cornelis' name is attached to the origin story;
- loss of Tanneken's operational continuity.

### Required tempo

Do **not** write this as an instant universal boycott or the overnight fall of a modern 'trading empire'. The intended movement is **erosion**:

one hesitation -> reduced trust -> harder credit / fewer options -> cumulative pressure.

The exact legal and commercial mechanics remain available for further historical reconstruction.

### Riskier text routes later

Cornelis already belongs to the Huis der Liefde network before the fire. Post-fire economic pressure may later:

- increase his dependence on trusted non-public networks;
- alter what risks he accepts;
- make clandestine transport work relatively more valuable;
- tighten the overlap between commerce and dangerous textual circulation.

It must **not** become the simplistic chain `poverty -> Protestant smuggler`.

## 9. Zierikzee -> Reimerswaal

Before the fire the intended continuation of Claes' schooling is **Zierikzee**.

After the fire, the combination of household loss, damaged capital, recovery needs and worsening economic/reputational pressure makes that plan financially or practically unattainable. Claes therefore continues his education in **Reimerswaal**, while Cornelis remains in Goes.

Claes is **eleven** when he arrives later in 1554 and turns twelve on 8 December. Any eight- or nine-year arrival note belongs to the superseded 1545-birth chronology.

The exact comparative price of schooling/boarding in Zierikzee versus Reimerswaal remains a historical research question. The **causal story function** is fixed even if the precise cost mechanism is later refined.

This produces the durable chain:

**Goes/Puttus -> intended Zierikzee -> fire and post-fire damage -> Reimerswaal.**

The manuscript has already planted Reimerswaal as the destination one would not want to inhabit, let alone attend school in. When the route changes, *De Kade* must return briefly and specifically to that remembered judgement. Generic cold/water unease is insufficient because it does not cash the earlier reader promise.

## 10. Protection read as rejection

Cornelis' post-fire choice has a binding double truth.

For Cornelis:

- preserve Claes' education;
- remove him from immediate ruin and rumour;
- keep him away from daily accusations attached to his father's name;
- remain in Goes himself to rebuild livelihood, credit and shelter.

For Claes:

> **Mother and Jan are dead, and now father sends me away too.**

Cornelis expresses love primarily through provision, arrangement, responsibility and protection. Claes longs for proximity, words and visible choosing. The same act is therefore both paternal sacrifice and filial experience of abandonment.

Claes should receive evidence of continuing care — money, materials, messages, arrangements or visits where plausible — without that evidence automatically healing the emotional reading.

Grandfather Claes Jacobsz. belongs visibly to this aftermath. He has lost the house he owned, is present by the burial/early formal arrangements and helps Cornelis preserve Claes' education. Temporary lodging with grandfather's sister may remain, but it needs a practical explanation and cannot function as an accidental erasure of grandfather.

## 11. Mayken as a different Goese memory

Mayken and Claes know one another before the fire without childhood-romance framing. Their fire histories diverge: Mayken's Lampert world suffers documented material destruction around the Zwaene, but her household is not annihilated in the same way as Claes'.

Because Claes leaves while Mayken remains connected to a different Goese household and rebuilding memory, she can later possess **partial local knowledge that Claes lacks**.

After their later reunion she may contribute fragments about:

- what people said about Cornelis;
- how rumour changed over time;
- what Cornelis tried to rebuild;
- what others saw him pay, arrange or sacrifice for Claes;
- why sending Claes away could also have been protective.

### Guardrail: no retrospective oracle

Mayken does not recover omniscient truth. Her knowledge comes through her own observation, family memory and local social transmission. She cannot prove the technical cause of the fire or know every private motive of Cornelis.

The strongest function comes **after Cornelis' death**: Claes cannot reconcile with his father directly, but he can revise the story he has carried about him. This is posthumous rereading, not retrospective absolution.

## 12. The metal Nissepat voetboog sign

Before the fire Claes knows a small metal **penning/hanger on a thin copper chain** bearing the Nissepat voetboog family sign within the fictional family/Sint-Joris material world. Grandfather Claes Jacobsz. had a small set made, and Jan receives this example after asking for one. It is not an ancient heirloom, official guild badge, schutterskoning prize or the object behind the historical Muschart record.

Do not use any of those labels as established historical fact until the form is decided and supported.

### Fixed fire phase

The object is present in the family/grandfather-household world and survives the fire **damaged**:

- blackened/sooted;
- discoloured;
- possibly slightly warped or scarred;
- still recognizable through the voetboog form.

It is not a pristine providential relic.

Its first post-fire function is simple:

> **this existed.**

The object can later resonate with documents, testimony, the memoriaal and other carriers that outlive people or states, but it must be used sparingly and without explanatory symbolism.

## 13. Women and labour in the zoutkeet world — research boundary

Current development deliberately rejects an automatic all-male salt-work scene. Comparative early-modern evidence and current research leads make female labour, female household-economic participation and women with property/investment roles important questions for the Zeeland salt world.

However the project has **not yet source-fixed** for Goes in 1554:

- `keetmeesteres` as the correct local title;
- `kopvrouw` with a precise package of rights;
- exact female tasks inside Cornelis' type of keet;
- ages of girls entering salt work;
- clothing such as breeches/bare feet;
- exact ownership percentages or female guild position.

Therefore women and girls may be designed into the fictional labour world where historically plausible, but specific institutional claims require verification before they become hard historical prose.

Tanneken's new role does **not** depend on proving she holds a formal salt-work title.

## 14. The event as micro-Nigredo

The alchemical architecture remains author-side. No child or narrator needs to call the fire `Nigredo`.

Its factual story operation is enough:

**before:** household, sibling equilibrium, expectation, property, routes, status, planned schooling  
**fire:** disintegration faster than understanding  
**after:** mother/brother/unborn child dead; house lost; economic system damaged; father's name compromised; father and son separated; intended future rerouted.

The fire is therefore not merely backstory trauma. It is the hinge that turns Claes' childhood forms of knowing into long-term questions about evidence, responsibility, uncertainty, testimony and release.
