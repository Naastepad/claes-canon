# SRC-HIST-AGRICOLA-DE-RE-METALLICA-1556-VITRIOL-001 — Agricola on vitriolic pyrites and green vitriol

**Type:** primary early-modern metallurgical source  
**Author:** Georgius Agricola  
**Work:** *De re metallica*  
**First edition:** Basel, 1556  
**Scope used here:** vitriolic pyrites, water-leaching, green solution, concentration and green-vitriol crystallisation.

## Evidence used

Agricola describes ores/material rich in **vitriolic pyrites** being exposed to dripping water or treated with hot water so that vitriol and related soluble matter are freed. He then describes the resulting **green solution** being transferred to other vats/tanks for clarification, boiled in lead vessels until concentrated, and finally allowed to condense/crystallise on rods/reeds/twigs as **green vitriol**.

This is strong period support for the process family used in the Claes reconstruction:

`pyritic/vitriolic matter -> water exposure/leaching -> green vitriolic liquor -> concentration -> green vitriol crystals`.

## What this source does not prove

- It does not identify the fictionally used Boom clay/waste as the exact material in Agricola's process.
- It does not prove Dee or Silvius personally used Agricola's procedure in Antwerp.
- It does not prove the complete projectiepoeder sequence.
- It does not make the novel's pyrite-to-vitriol route a recovered laboratory recipe for Claes.
