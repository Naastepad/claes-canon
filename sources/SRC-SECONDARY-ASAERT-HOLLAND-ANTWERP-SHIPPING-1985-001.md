- **Author:** Gustaaf Asaert, rijksarchivaris

- **Publication:** *Neerlandia*, jaargang 89 (1985), pp. 103-114
- **Consulted representation:** DBNL

## Evidence used

### Holland-Antwerp shipping

Asaert describes Hollanders as traders and transporters regularly present in Antwerp and notes Delft among the Holland towns whose skippers visited the Scheldt port in the earlier evidence. For the mid-sixteenth century he cites research by M. Cassiers on 1555-1558: Hollanders accounted for a very large share of non-Antwerp maritime traffic, including 40% of the reported oversea traffic in that study. In a sample of 494 grain-delivery vessels, 469 were northern Netherlandish.

Asaert also records a 1548 protest by the deans of the Antwerp skippers concerning Charles V's southbound Scheldt shipping ordinance and feared advantages for Hollanders.

Primary locator for that protest: Stadsarchief Antwerpen, `Pk 1.012`, `Informatie genomen aen... dekens... van den schippers... nopende der schipvaert (1548)`, par. 66.

### Beer-distribution contract comparator, 1446

Asaert gives a detailed example from 21 July 1446 between Antwerp distributor Jan de Hertoge and Gouda skipper Jan Heyden. The skipper delivered Gouda beer at Antwerp's Bierhoofd; the Antwerp party handled local distribution. The arrangement included:

- payment per vat sold;
- collection and return of empty casks;
- separate compensation linked to returned empties;
- working capital advanced by the supplier/shipper side;
- repayment terms when the relationship ended;
- compensation for missing casks.

Primary locator: Stadsarchief Antwerpen, Schepenregister 37, fol. 57r.

## Claes use

The mid-sixteenth-century evidence establishes that Holland-Antwerp vessel traffic was a normal and substantial commercial corridor. The 1446 beer agreement is valuable only as a **mechanism comparator** showing that commission, reusable-cask logistics, working capital and missing-cask liability were historically real elements of Holland-to-Antwerp beer distribution.

## Boundaries

- The 1446 contract is more than a century before the Horen-De Hont act of 1589. None of its exact prices, commission rates, repayment periods or liabilities may be copied into the 1589 agreement or Cornelis' contract as historical fact.
- The 1555-1558 traffic shares are broad Holland/Antwerp evidence, not De Hont-specific evidence.
- Delft's earlier presence in Antwerp does not prove a specific De Hont voyage.
