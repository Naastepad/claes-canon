## Evidence used

The article describes Delft's artificial-waterway network and the 1389 Delft-controlled connection from Overschie to the Maas at Delfshaven. It reports a 1548 process memorandum by Delft pensionary Willem Criep stating that beer schuiten had long been pulled along the Schie toward the Maas `mit den halse`.

For 1551 it states that Hague beer carriers obtained permission to use horses between Delft and The Hague because they no longer wished to pull their **heavy schuiten carrying Delft beer** by human force in all weather.

## Claes use

This gives period-specific support for three practical authoring facts:

1. Delft beer moved in dedicated/heavily laden schuit traffic rather than only in tiny hand-carried lots.
2. Canal/tow logistics were part of Delft's beer-distribution infrastructure before and during Cornelis' adult life.
3. Delft's commercial water system connected the city to its Maas-side seaport at Delfshaven.

## Boundaries

- This source does not identify Aernouldt de Hont.
- The Hague route is not the Goes route.
- `zware schuiten` is a functional description, not an exact historical vessel class or capacity.
- It does not establish that the same inland beer schuit continued from Delft across open/tidal waters to Goes or Antwerp.
- Exact transshipment practice for De Hont remains open.
