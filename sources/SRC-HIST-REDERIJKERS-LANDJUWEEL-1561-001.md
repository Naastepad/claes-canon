## Chamber as working institution

Rederijkerskamers are not merely theatre clubs. In the project they should be treated as urban corporations and communication networks joining vernacular composition, performance, civic sociability, devotion, competition and public reputation.

Scene-safe activities supported by the wider rederijker record include:

- composing and correcting verse;
- internal rhetorical exercise/instruction;
- preparing and rehearsing `spelen van sinne` and `esbattementen`;
- refreinen, songs and blazon-related work;
- civic/religious appearances and processional display;
- travel and interurban competition;
- meals, drink, status and administrative work around performances.

Specific offices such as prince, headman, dean and factor are historical institutional roles. They must **not** be silently assigned to Cornelis; his deken status remains open and factor/prince are not current canon.

## Antwerp Landjuweel 1561

The 1562 Willem Silvius print is exceptionally valuable contemporary evidence. Its title states that **fourteen chambers** presented themselves at the Antwerp Landjuweel on 3 August 1561. The book contains the charter, a short description of the chambers' entries, contest results, the Violieren blazon and the plays of sinne.

Modern scholarship identifies the event as the last great Brabantine landjuweel sequence, organised by Antwerp's Violieren. The surviving print and later documentary studies support a world of formal entries, blazons, mounted and wheeled display, music, rhetoric, contests, judging and civic reception.

## Hard participation boundary

No current evidence in the project proves that a Goese chamber was one of the fourteen official competitors or won a prize at Antwerp in 1561. Therefore:

- Claes and Cornelis may fictionally travel to Antwerp, observe, meet people and learn through the event under existing canon;
- do not give Goes an official competing entry, assigned order, prize or documented procession unless new evidence establishes it.

Dee is not placed in Antwerp in 1561.

## Narrative mechanism

The core developmental lesson for Claes is:

`sign → performance → audience → competing interpretation → social consequence`

He learns that the same object, role, costume or words can function differently for performers, judges, authorities, printers and spectators.
