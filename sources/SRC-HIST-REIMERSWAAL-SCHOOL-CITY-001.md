# SRC-HIST-REIMERSWAAL-SCHOOL-CITY-001 — Reimerswaal as functioning school city under pressure

**Type:** historical urban/education source bundle  
**Status:** LOCAL_SOURCE_ANCHORS_PLUS_EXPLICIT_RECONSTRUCTION  
**Scope:** Reimerswaal 1554–1561 and school continuity relevant to Claes' cost-pupil years.

## City state during Claes' years

Reimerswaal must not be written as an abandoned ruin in 1554. It remained a functioning Zeeland town with salt refining, madder/wool-related economic activity and trade connections while recurrent floods and fire damaged its built environment and confidence.

Scene-changing local anchors during Claes' stay include:

- 1555: flood/storm damage;
- 1557: severe inundation/storm damage affecting walls/gates and urban buildings and salt works;
- 1558: major fire;
- 1561: renewed flooding.

These events are not a mandate to stage four disaster spectacles. Their main authoring value is cumulative: routes change, repairs persist, buildings disappear or are patched, household calculations change, and water becomes an ordinary threat.

## School evidence

The existence of an old urban/parochial school tradition is historically supported:

- the *Encyclopedie van Zeeland* states Reimerswaal had a school before 1296;
- the surviving 1497 regest records the replacement of one `scolaster` by a singing master and a schoolmaster appointed by the civic government with advice of the pastor;
- the Reimerswaal archive inventory records agreements to appoint schoolmasters in 1569/1570, including Pauwels Verhagen on 11 March 1570.

This establishes durable school infrastructure across the period. It does **not by itself prove the exact 1554 curriculum, classroom layout, rectoral hierarchy or every Latin-school practice used in Claes' fictional education**.

## Novel reconstruction boundary

Novel canon places Claes in Reimerswaal from 1554 to summer 1561 for Latin formation after elementary schooling in Goes. Treat this as a source-weighted fictional reconstruction built on the documented long-lived school tradition. Do not claim that a surviving source names Claes, his rector, his host family or his exact course of study.

Transferable urban Latin-school practices — oral recitation, grammar, memorisation, copying, correction, older pupils assisting younger pupils and cost/boarding arrangements — may be used only when marked as comparative reconstruction rather than direct Reimerswaal proof.

## Material and sensory environment

Useful local fields include salt works and brine/smoke, tidal water and mud, damp timber/masonry, repair work, market/harbour movement, church bells and song, parchment/paper/ink at school, cold/wet clothes and the contrast between a damaged object that remains readable and papers destroyed by fire.

Claes' water-damaged Zovitius schoolbook is a fictional object biography and a thematic counterpoint to the Goes fire; historical floods support the environment, not the specific object's provenance.

## Writing guardrails

1. Functioning city under pressure, not picturesque ruin.
2. No invented named rector or fixed school building unless separately sourced/decided.
3. No seven years of beginner Latin; Claes progresses into older-pupil/advanced formation.
4. Do not claim the exact 1554 curriculum as archival fact.
5. Disasters should alter ordinary life cumulatively rather than become repetitive set pieces.
6. Preserve distinction between historically documented school continuity and fictional Claes attendance/curriculum.
