# SRC-HIST-SCHIEDAM-KROMSTEVEN-1578-1595-001 — Schiedam ship transactions, 1578–1595

- **Source ID:** `SRC-HIST-SCHIEDAM-KROMSTEVEN-1578-1595-001`
- **Source type:** municipal archival finding-aid / regest series based on the old judicial archive of Schiedam
- **Repository:** Gemeentearchief Schiedam
- **Consulted records:** *Nadere toegang 70* (Giftboek, 4 January 1578–2 June 1584; archive Gerechten van Schiedam, inv. 328) and *Nadere toegang 72* (Giftboek, 31 March 1590–15 August 1595)
- **Verification date:** 2026-08-30
- **Use in Claes:** near-contemporary vessel-type and ship-finance comparators for the Holland–Zeeland commercial water network; not evidence for Aernouldt de Hont's actual vessel.

## Kromstevenschuit, 1582

Entry 623, fol. 221v, 14 July 1582 records ship carpenter Euwout Cornelisz of Overschie selling Pieter Jansz a **kromstevenschuit, groot 3 last**, newly launched, for 57 guilders of 40 groten Vlaams. Twelve guilders were paid cash and the balance by instalments; the vessel and buyer's person were bound under `waterrecht`.

This proves that the named vessel type was commercially real in Holland well inside Claes' lifetime and could be expressed in a `last` capacity designation.

### Capacity boundary

The `3 last` in this ship transaction is a vessel/cargo-capacity statement. It must **not** be silently converted into a number of beer barrels by applying a beer-trade `last` definition from another place or source. `Last` was commodity- and locality-sensitive.

## Additional 1590s evidence

The 1590–1595 giftbook contains repeated transactions for `kromsteven schuit(en)`, including buyers or sureties tied to Brielle, Brouwershaven, Delfshaven, Vlaardingen and other waterborne places. This shows the type was not an isolated lexical occurrence.

Most useful for the Claes geography is entry 794, fol. 728, 1 February 1594: Joost Engelsz Mol, son of the bailiff of **Remmerswaal**, owed the final instalment on a newly built kromstevenschuit, with a surety constituted before the burgomasters and schepenen of Remmerswaal. The following entry concerns ironwork for that same vessel.

This gives a direct late-sixteenth-century **Reimerswaal connection** to the type.

## Authoring boundary

These records support `KROMSTEVEN_SCHUIT` as a strong regional analogue for a small/medium commercial schuit in Claes' world. They do not prove:

- that De Hont owned one;
- that De Hont sailed one in 1589;
- that a 3-last kromsteven could carry a fixed number of Delft beer tons;
- that every Goes–Delft or Goes–Antwerp movement used this type.

Use vessel type as an authoring analogue unless a De Hont-specific source is found.
