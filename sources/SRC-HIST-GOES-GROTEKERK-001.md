## Material verified for this record
Verified on 13 August 2026 from the full Erfgoed van Goes text supplied by the author and screenshots of the linked StoryMap supplied in the same research session.

The material supports, among other points:
- a new choir begun in 1455; the StoryMap dates the surviving choir phase 1455–1475;
- north and south transepts built around 1500; the StoryMap dates them 1500–1508;
- a Catholic interior before 1578 with roughly thirty large and small altars, including a central high altar, sacrament tower, sacrament altar, Gospel and Epistle lecterns, a seat for the pastor, and guild chapels/altars;
- a renewed Our Lady chapel in the northern choir closure and a **probable** Maria Magdalena shrine/chapel in the southern choir closure; Maria Magdalena is explicitly described as also being venerated by the Goes rhetoricians;
- the Maria Magdalena chapel shown in the StoryMap with the 1552 triptych for Matthijs van der Straten;
- an older blockwerk organ followed in 1550 by an organ built by father and son Niehoff, situated in the corner of the choir and north transept; the StoryMap popup describes the displayed position as the location in 1555;
- the Institute of the Seven Hours established in 1440, with daily psalm singing at seven fixed times, and Catholic liturgical practice ending with the Reformation in 1578;
- saint paintings and guild-related wall paintings before 1578, later whitewashed or otherwise removed from view;
- the St Gregory Mass relief in the north transept, damaged during the iconoclastic period and later hidden behind a wall;
- pest grooves in the north-transept facade, a punishment bench, anchorite cells/kluizen with fifteenth-century occupants, a baptismal chapel, and a c. 1525 ancillary room labelled “Consistorie” in the StoryMap;
- StoryMap reconstruction of the four crossing pillars as Gregorius (NW), Ambrosius (SW), Augustinus (SE) and Hieronymus (NE);
- twelve choir pillars reconstructed with apostolic identities; the source wording says the pillars **probably** bore apostle names;
- a 1594 pulpit attached to a pillar in the choir;
- the 1618 fire and 1619–1621 rebuilding materially changed the nave, so the present/post-1618 nave must not be back-projected unchanged into Claes’ sixteenth-century church.

## Named altars in the nave
The supplied StoryMap text lists the following altars as having stood in the nave while stating that their precise positions are unknown:
- St Christopher altar of the beer carriers’ guild;
- St Apollonia altar of the sack carriers’ guild;
- Three Kings altar;
- St Barbara altar;
- St Anthony altar;
- Sweet Name of Jesus altar;
- St Peter altar;
- St Andrew altar;
- St Agnes altar;
- Holy Trinity altar.

The StoryMap also states that the late-medieval choir contained at least twelve guild chapels/altars. Except where the source explicitly establishes a location, marker placement must be treated as reconstruction rather than archival proof.

## Spatial reconstruction recorded from screenshots
The StoryMap’s apostolic sequence is useful for scene blocking but remains a reconstruction:
- northern/outer row, west → east: Petrus, Andreas, Jakobus de Oudere, Filippus;
- southern/inner row, west → east: Bartholomeus, Thomas, Jakobus de Jongere, Thadeus;
- southern/outer row, west → east: Simon, Judas (opgevolgd door Mattias), Mattheus, Johannes.

The StoryMap places the renewed Onze Lieve Vrouwekapel in the northern/eastern choir closure and the St Maria Magdalenakapel in the southern/eastern choir closure.

## Reliability and limitations
- Preserve the source’s own uncertainty words: **vermoedelijk**, **waarschijnlijk**, and statements that exact places are unknown.
- StoryMap pins can express the reconstruction chosen by its makers without proving that the exact historical position is archival fact.
- The connection between Maria Magdalena and the Goes rhetoricians is explicit, but no supplied evidence proves ownership, exclusive use or formal rights in the chapel by a specific chamber such as the Edele Castanienbloem.
- The beer carriers’ guild is not the same institution as the brewers; Cornelis’ fictional brewing/beer-trade role does not automatically make him a member of the beer carriers’ guild.
- Anchorites are documented for the fifteenth century; do not place a living anchorite in Claes’ youth without further evidence.
- The present nave largely reflects the rebuilding after the fire of 1618 and cannot be used uncritically for the 1542–1602 story period.
