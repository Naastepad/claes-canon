## Verified textual anchors
### Luke 8:5 — opening of the sower
Leuven 1548 begins: `Hy is wtghegaen die saeyt om sijn saet te saeyen...`

Liesvelt 1542 begins: `Het ghinc een saeyman wt te saeyen sijn saet...`

The narrative content is the same, but the lexical/syntactic wording is demonstrably different. Therefore dialogue or quoted Scripture must be tied to a specific edition rather than reconstructed from a modern translation.

### Luke 8:11 — interpretation
Leuven 1548: `Ende dit is die ghelijckenisse, Tsaet is dwoort Gods,`

Liesvelt 1542: `Dat is die gelijckenisse, Dat saet is dat woort Gods,`

Again the doctrinal core is the same while spelling, articles and phrasing differ.

## Narrative implications for Claes
- In a Catholic Mass, the normative Scriptural hearing remains Latin/Vulgate; neither Dutch wording should silently replace the liturgical Latin.
- A Dutch formulation belongs to an actual vernacular reading, teaching, preaching or remembered explanatory context.
- If the novel uses a Leuven Bible as a Catholic vernacular object, use the Leuven wording or explicitly modernise it in narration.
- If a Liesvelt Bible enters Goes, its wording and object provenance should remain confessionally/materially distinct; do not treat it as the ordinary Catholic Bible.
