# Van Bruaene — Om beters wille

Anne-Laure Van Bruaene, *Om beters wille. Rederijkerskamers en de stedelijke cultuur in de Zuidelijke Nederlanden (1400–1650)* (Amsterdam University Press, 2008).

Research use for Claes:
- rederijkerskamers were urban corporations specializing in public communication, drama and lyric;
- their activity belonged to civic festivities, processions, markets, peace celebrations, entries and interurban competitions;
- their social basis included substantial middling groups such as craftsmen and small traders;
- lay devotion was a central component of urban identity rather than a detachable religious add-on;
- chambers could combine civic religion, competition, mobility and public spectacle with increasingly interiorized and intellectualized religious reflection;
- rederijker networks could also become conduits for reform-minded and contested religious ideas;
- Antwerp's 1561 landjuweel and haagspel belong to this dense interurban network; the study documents participation and connections across chambers and regions.

Narrative relevance: use the chamber not merely as theatre backdrop but as a social technology linking craft, trade, civic identity, devotion, rhetoric, competition, networks and confessional tension. This strengthens the function of the 1561 Antwerp episode in Claes' development from sensory perception toward interpretation and public consequence.

Guardrail: general South-Netherlandish patterns do not prove a particular institutional practice in Goes or Reimerswaal. Local chamber names, membership, offices and events remain dependent on Zeeland-specific evidence.
