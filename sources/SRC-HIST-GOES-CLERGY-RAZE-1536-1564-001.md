# SRC-HIST-GOES-CLERGY-RAZE-1536-1564-001

Source basis: the user's normalized transcription workbook `RAZE-1745-1760-master-1533-1675.xlsx`, derived from Goese transport registers RAZE 1745, 1746 and 1749.

## Direct archival-transcription anchors

- **21 March 1536 — RAZE-1745-0112, fol. 33:** `mr. Mathijs Jacopsen pastoor` sells a quarter share in the house called *die Magdalene* near the monastery.
- **14 March 1541 — RAZE-1746-0015, fol. 36:** `mr. Mathijs Jacopsen, vice-pastoor` buys a house in the Langevorststraat.
- **27 June 1541 — RAZE-1746-0053, fol. 44:** `Mathijs Jacopsen pastoor` buys a house `aan het kerkhof`.
- **23 January 1542 — RAZE-1746-0126, fol. 63–63v:** `Mathijs Jacopsen pastoor` sells a house `achter het koor van de kerk`.
- **27 February 1542 — RAZE-1746-0138, fol. 66v:** `mr. Mathijs Jacobsen vice- cureyt ter Goes` buys a house in the Zuideinde, bounded in part by the Handbogenhof. This is the strongest direct proof of his local parochial function in Goes.
- **20 March 1564 — RAZE-1749-0154, fol. 94:** `Clemens van den Dale, licentiaat pastoor Goes` buys the house *de Lombaert* on the north side of the Grote Markt.

## Evidence conclusions

1. Mathijs Jacopsen/Jacobsen is a real Goese cleric, repeatedly called pastoor and, more precisely, vice-pastoor / vice-cureit in 1541–1542.
2. The phrase `vice-cureyt ter Goes` directly establishes a local cure-of-souls role in Goes on 27 February 1542.
3. His property transactions cluster around the church/kerkhof/choir environment, but **ownership is not proof of residence**.
4. The identification of this cure with the Maria Magdalenaparochie is strongly supported by the Goese parochial context and church geography, but the decisive 27 February 1542 act does not itself spell out `Maria Magdalena`.
5. The exact titular benefice-holder above the vice-cureit is not established by these transport acts.
6. The exact office-holder on **8 December 1542**, Claes' canonical birth date, is not established by the currently recovered acts. Mathijs is documented in office in the birth year, but must not be asserted as the priest present at Claes' birth or baptism without further evidence.
7. The succession between the last recovered Mathijs evidence in 1542 and Clemens van den Dale in 1564 remains a research gap.

## Storybible use

Mathijs may serve as the named historical clergy anchor for the Catholic Goese world immediately preceding and entering Claes' birth year. Clemens may serve as the later documented Goese pastor anchor in 1564. Neither name by itself creates a fictional encounter with Claes.
