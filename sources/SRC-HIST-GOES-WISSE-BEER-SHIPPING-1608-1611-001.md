# SRC-HIST-GOES-WISSE-BEER-SHIPPING-1608-1611-001 — Goes shipping and Delft-beer mechanisms, 1608–1611

- **Source type:** annotated extracts from the Goese notarial register RAZE 2043
- **Work:** P.A. Harthoorn / M.P. Smallegange, *Jacob Boudewijn Wisse — Notaris in Goes. Geannoteerde extracten uit RAZE 2043 (1608–1611)*
- **Primary record family:** GOES, RAZE 2043, Notaris Jacob Boudewijnsz Wisse
- **Verification date:** 2026-08-30
- **Use in Claes:** near-contemporary mechanism comparator only. These records are six to nine years after Claes' 1602 endpoint and must never be represented as events Claes necessarily witnessed.

## Delfshaven–Goes vessel transactions

### 26 August 1608 — kromsteven schuit

RAZE 2043, opening record: Jan Daemen, **schipper van Delfshaven**, sold Jan Cornelisz Palinckman, citizen and schipper of Goes, a `cromsteven schuytte` with its equipment for £29:3:4 groten Vlaams.

This gives a direct Delfshaven–Goes commercial connection involving a kromsteven schuit shortly after the Claes period.

### 27 August 1609 — crabbeschuyt

RAZE 2043, fol. 152: Maerten Jansz, **schipper te Delfshaven**, while present in Goes acknowledged 345 Carolus guilders debt to Jan Cornelisz Palinckman for purchase and delivery of a `crabbeschuyt` with all equipment.

This gives a second concrete small-craft type in the same Holland–Goes corridor.

## Delft beer in the Goes hinterland, 1609

### 10 April 1609 — Delft beer on credit

RAZE 2043, fol. 87: Pieter Cornelisz, innkeeper at 's-Heer Abtskerke, acknowledged a debt to Jan Cornelisz, `biersteker van den rinck`, arising from purchase and delivery of **Delft beers** already received. The debt was secured using another obligation.

This is useful evidence for a chain in which Delft beer reached the Goes/South-Beveland market through a named local biersteker and could be distributed on credit.

### 25 April 1609 — sale by the ton

RAZE 2043, fol. 88: witnesses stated that a broker sold a `Last Delfs bier` in the name of the biersteker `van den rinck` at **18 schellingen per tonne**. The buyer could take one, two, more or fewer tons as desired.

This demonstrates flexible ton-by-ton local distribution rather than requiring a customer to take an entire incoming shipload.

## Delft brewer -> Goese schippers -> debt collection

RAZE 2043, fol. 207, 8 November 1609 records Goese schippers Jacob Quirijnsz and Stoffel Jacobsz acting pursuant to a 28 October 1608 procuration from Henrick Dirricxsz Bugge and Cornelis Machielsz, brewer in `de drije haringhen` at Delft, empowering agents in Goes to collect debts in the land of Goes and elsewhere.

This is a strong near-contemporary comparator for a commercial topology in which shipping, local distribution and credit collection overlap without requiring the shipper, brewer and biersteker to be the same person.

## Weather / navigational risk comparator

The same register opens with an August 1608 testimony about a Goese skipper sailing from the head of Goes toward Arnemuiden whose vessel capsized in a strong squall despite attempts to save it. Passengers drowned.

This supports weather exposure as a real operational risk on short Zeeland water legs. It does **not** establish the contractual allocation of cargo loss between shipper, biersteker, brewer or merchant.

## Guardrails

1. These 1608–1611 records are **NEAR_CONTEMPORARY**, not Claes-period direct evidence.
2. Do not identify either the kromsteven or krabschuit as De Hont's vessel.
3. Do not project the 1609 Delft-beer price of 18 schellingen/tonne backward to 1589 or 1560.
4. Do not infer De Hont's freight rate or cargo-risk terms from these later acts.
5. What they support is the **mechanism family**: Holland/Delft shipping -> Goes/South-Beveland biersteker distribution -> credit/debt -> local customers.
