## Relevant evidence

For the fifteenth- and sixteenth-century Southern Low Countries, Dreijer distinguishes:

- **freight** as a fixed payment;
- foreseeable common operational costs such as ordinary pilotage and port dues, increasingly allocated in freight contracts;
- **particular average**, where an individual interested party bears a loss;
- **general average**, where extraordinary sacrifices/costs made for the common safety of the maritime venture are shared among the interested parties.

The sixteenth-century regulatory trend also moved toward stricter liability for the shipmaster in cases of preventable damage, while giving the master operational discretion in genuine danger. The Habsburg maritime ordinances of 1551 and 1563 are important points in that development.

## Project use

This source supports an **authoring contract model** for Cornelis–De Hont in which freight, ordinary voyage costs, cargo/commercial risk, preventable carrier fault and extraordinary common peril are not collapsed into one undifferentiated `transport risk`.

## Guardrails

1. This is a Southern-Low-Countries/Antwerp legal-commercial framework, not a recovered Cornelis–De Hont contract.
2. Do not state that every short Goes voyage used a formal general-average declaration.
3. Exact local customary liability, insurance, pilotage and port-cost clauses remain scene- and contract-specific.
4. Marine insurance may exist in the wider Antwerp world but is not the default assumption for Cornelis' routine regional consignments.
