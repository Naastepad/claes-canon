## Why this source matters

De Vries' Table 2 combines wage evidence from ten locations in the western Netherlands (Holland, Zeeland and Utrecht) and seven locations in the eastern Netherlands. The text explicitly states that the post-1570 wage development discussed in the chapter is summarized in these unweighted multi-location indices.

For **Goes**, the source apparatus gives the underlying archival provenance as:

> `G.A. Goes, Rekeningen van de stad, no. 783-959; rekeningen stadsfabriek, no. 1813-14, 1793-97.`

This is the strongest recovered pointer so far to the municipal records underlying a historical Goese civil/construction wage series relevant to Claes' period.

## Evidence boundary

The currently accessible text transcription preserves the **source list** for Tables 2a/2b but does **not preserve the numeric Table 2 rows themselves in readable text**. Therefore this source currently establishes:

- that Goes is one of the western-Netherlands wage locations used by De Vries;
- the exact municipal archive ranges cited for Goes;
- that the data belong to the public/construction/manual-labour evidence family discussed in the chapter;
- the regional pattern that nominal wages rose strongly after the 1570s.

It does **not yet establish an exact Goese daily wage for a specific year** in the Claes period. Those values must be recovered from the original table image/data file or re-extracted from the cited Goese accounts.

## Regional comparator — not a Goes value

De Vries gives detailed wage steps for the Rijnland drainage works at Spaarndam/Halfweg: craftsmen move from 7 stuivers before 1565 to 9 in 1565, 12 (occasionally 15) in 1578, 14 in 1589, 16 in 1590 and 18 in 1593; unskilled wages move from 5 to 6 in 1565, to 9 by 1583 and eventually 14 by 1606. These observations demonstrate the broader western-Netherlands wage acceleration but must remain **regional comparator evidence**, never a substituted Goese series.

The chapter also cautions that before the Revolt nominal wages were sticky and that daily wage, annual earnings and cost of living are separate concepts.

## Writing / resolver guardrails

1. Do not label Spaarndam/Halfweg, Holland-wide or western-Netherlands numbers as `Goes`.
2. Do not derive a precise Goese wage merely from De Vries' statement that western urban/rural wage levels were broadly integrated.
3. Treat the listed G.A. Goes inventory ranges as **provenance for targeted extraction**, not as already extracted numeric observations.
4. Daily wage is not annual earnings; working days, seasonality, employment continuity and in-kind elements matter.
5. Until the numerical Goese observations are recovered, keep `GAP.ECON.GOES.WAGES.1540_1602` open.
