# SRC-SECONDARY-DEVRIES-LABOUR-MARKET-GOES-WAGES-001 — Goese wage-series provenance and coverage boundary

- **Source ID:** `SRC-SECONDARY-DEVRIES-LABOUR-MARKET-GOES-WAGES-001`
- **Source type:** secondary labour-market synthesis with explicit archival source apparatus
- **Author of relevant chapter:** Jan de Vries
- **Chapter:** `IV — The Labour Market`
- **Consulted representation:** public full-text transcription hosted by ResearchGate; parallel Scribd transcription used as cross-check
- **Verification date:** 2026-08-30

## What De Vries actually establishes

De Vries states that Table 2 summarizes wage evidence from ten locations in the western Netherlands (Holland, Zeeland and Utrecht) and seven in the eastern Netherlands. For **Goes**, the source apparatus gives:

> `G.A. Goes, Rekeningen van de stad, no. 783-959; rekeningen stadsfabriek, no. 1813-14, 1793-97.`

This proves that De Vries used Goese municipal material in the western-Netherlands wage compilation. It does **not by itself date those inventory numbers to Claes' lifetime**.

## Critical archive-coverage correction

A separate Zeeland repertory supplies a date cross-check for the same archive and cites:

- `GA Goes, Stad Goes, inv.nr. 784 (folio 25r.)`

for Jan van Nieuwenhuijse noting strangers during the Goes annual market in **1627**.

That cross-check means the De Vries range `783-959` must **not** be described as though it were automatically a 1540-1602 series. At least one inventory number at the beginning of the cited range is used for a 1627 record. A dated inventory mapping of the whole range is required before assigning its wage observations to any story year.

The project's earlier wording that this was the strongest pointer to municipal records `relevant to Claes' period` was therefore too strong and is superseded by this boundary.

## Numeric Table 2 limitation

The currently accessible transcription preserves the **source list** for Tables 2a/2b but does not expose a readable numeric Goese Table 2 row. Consequently it currently establishes:

- Goes as an actual source location in De Vries' western wage compilation;
- De Vries' cited Goese archive ranges;
- a public/construction/manual-labour source family;
- broader western-Netherlands post-1570 wage acceleration.

It does **not** establish a defensible exact Goese daily wage for **1540-1602**. The next local target is a dated pre-1602 Goese account series or another direct local wage source, not an interpolation from this inventory range.

## Mid-sixteenth-century regional comparator — not a Goes value

De Vries' Table 1 does provide direct day-wage observations for 1550–1554 in other cities. Two useful comparators are:

- **Bergen op Zoom:** skilled construction labour 5.5–6 stuivers/day; unskilled labour 3–4 stuivers/day;
- **Antwerp:** skilled construction labour 9 stuivers/day; unskilled labour 4 stuivers/day.

These figures are useful as bounded regional/commercial comparators during Claes' youth. They also show why one generic `Netherlands wage` is unsafe: skilled rates differ substantially by city even within the same period. They remain **C_REGIONAL_PROXY**, not reconstructed Goese observations.

## Post-1570 regional comparator — not a Goes value

For the Rijnland drainage works at Spaarndam/Halfweg, De Vries describes craftsmen at 7 stuivers through 1565, 9 in 1565, 12 (occasionally 15) in 1578, 14 in 1589, 16 in 1590 and 18 in 1593. Unskilled wages move from 5 to 6 in 1565, to 9 by 1583 and eventually 14 by 1606. This demonstrates the scale of western-Netherlands wage acceleration, not a Goese wage path.

De Vries also stresses that before the Revolt nominal wages were sticky, that real wages declined in much of the first sixty years of the sixteenth century, and that daily wage, annual earnings and cost of living are separate concepts.

## Writing / resolver guardrails

1. Do not label Bergen op Zoom, Antwerp, Spaarndam/Halfweg or western-Netherlands numbers as `Goes`.
2. Do not infer a precise Goese wage from regional labour-market integration.
3. Do not treat De Vries' `783-959` range as 1540-1602 until a dated archive inventory proves that coverage.
4. Daily wage is not annual earnings; working days, seasonality, employment continuity and in-kind elements matter.
5. Use the 1550–1554 comparator values only as plausibility checks while preserving city and skill class.
6. Keep `GAP.ECON.GOES.WAGES.1540_1602` open until a direct, dated pre-1602 Goese observation is recovered.
