## Exact locators used

### Roer / Schutten
The section beginning **“Corte onderwysinghe op de figuerliicke afbeeldinghe, belanghende t'recht ghebruyck des Roers”** states that the written instruction corresponds by number to each figure. The section concludes with **“De woorden van commandement”**, explicitly describing the words by which captains command their soldiers in sequence.

The roer sequence contains **42 numbered figures/commands**. The command list includes, among others:

- `1. Op u schouder u Roer wel hout ende Marcheert.`
- `5. U lonte inde rechter handt neemt.`
- `7. U lont opdruct.`
- `8. U lont versoect.`
- `9. U lont afblaest ende u pan opent.`
- `10. U Roer aenlegt.`
- `11. Schiet.`
- `16. Op u pan polver doet.`
- `23. U Roer ladet.`
- `26. U polver neer stampt.`
- `42. U pan deckt ende ghereet staet.`

The accompanying prose makes clear that figure 8 concerns testing/adjusting the match, not a command equivalent to the later expression “geeft acht”.

The general command is **“U loop om hooch hout”**: commanders are to train soldiers habitually to keep the muzzle upward to prevent accidents.

### Musquetier
The section **“Corte onderrechtinghe ... het recht ghebruyck van Musquet”** contains **43 numbered figures/commands**. The additional complexity derives especially from managing the heavier musket together with the `furquet` (rest/fork).

### Spiesdrager
The pike/spies section contains a separate numbered drill sequence of **32 figures/commands**. It is a distinct weapon-specific vocabulary rather than a shortened firearm loading sequence.

## What this source supports

1. De Gheyn's work is not merely a picture book: numbered figures, detailed prose instruction and an explicit list of command words are integrated.
2. The command sentence is the operational verbal unit; the figure number is the correspondence/indexing mechanism between image and explanation. The source does **not** by itself prove that officers shouted “Ten achtste!” as the battlefield command.
3. The firearm sequence shown is cyclic. The initial figures assume a soldier with a weapon already prepared/loaded; firing is followed by pan safety/priming and then reloading of the main barrel.
4. The source distinguishes a small priming/touch-powder flask (`cleyn flesken met laedt polver`) from the flask/charges used for the main load.
5. The work codifies a highly explicit, repeatable and safety-conscious drill: match handling, pan management, muzzle direction, presentation, firing, reloading and return to readiness are decomposed into discrete actions.

## Limitations / guardrails

- The work dates from 1607 and codifies the mature Mauritian/States drill; it must not be projected unchanged onto Goes in 1550, 1572 or immediately after the 1577 satisfactie.
- The source does not identify its engraved figures as soldiers from Goes, Zeeland, or any specific regiment.
- It does not prove that Goes was a model garrison, experimental drill centre or direct source for De Gheyn's models.
- Applicability to a Goese urban militia, garrison and field-army unit must be kept distinct unless local evidence bridges those institutions.
- “Geeft acht” must not be derived from figure/command 8. Figure 8 is `U lont versoect` / “Try your match”.
