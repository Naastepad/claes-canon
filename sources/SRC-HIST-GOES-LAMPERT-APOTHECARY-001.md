# SRC-HIST-GOES-LAMPERT-APOTHECARY-001 — Goese apothekerskring Lampart/Lambert/Lampert

**Status:** historical source dossier  
**Scope:** Goes, ca. 1538–1564  
**Use:** historical anchoring for the fictional beloved Mayken Adriaensdr. Lampert. Historical persons, fictional genealogy and unresolved identity links must remain separate.

## Directly attested records

### Jacob/Jacop Lampart/Lambert, apotheker
- `RAZE-1745-0249`, 20 May 1538: **Jacob Lampart den apotheker** sells a house and hof aan de Veste.
- `RAZE-1745-0334`, 16 June 1539: **Jacop de apotheker met Merricken zijn huisvrouw** transfers half a house at the Beestenmarkt.
- `RAZE-1746-0205`, 12 December 1542: **Merricken huisvrouw Jacop de apoheker** buys the house **Den Eenhoorn** at the west side of the stadhuis.
- `RAZE-1746-0278`, 9 July 1543: **Mayken huisvrouw Jacop Lampart** sells a house at the Molendijk.
- `RAZE-1746-0472` and `RAZE-1746-0473`, 30 June 1545: **Merricken huisvrouw Jacob Lambert apotheker** buys a house and small house at the Oprel beside the Gasthuis.

The records establish an apothecary named Jacob/Jacop Lampart/Lambert and female household-name forms Merricken and Mayken in the immediate Lampart environment. They do **not** yet prove whether the 1543 Mayken and the 1539/1542/1545 Merricken are the same woman, variant name forms, successive wives, or different same-name households.

### Adriaen Jacopsen / Adriaen Jacopsen Lampert
- `RAZE-1747-0579`, 22 June 1551: **Adriaen Jacopsen Lampert** transfers a house at the west side of the stadhuis.
- `RAZE-1748-0110`, 16 January 1553: **Adriaen Jacopsen Lampert** buys **die Nyen Zwaene**, north side Grote Markt at the corner of the Oprel.
- `RAZE-1748-0224`, 12 March 1554: **Adriaen Jacopsen apteker** buys a house in the Lange Vorststraat; his hof is the east boundary.
- `RAZE-1748-0307`, 28 January 1555: **Adriaen Jacopsen Lampert** sells a **verbrand huis genaamd de Zwaene** on the north side of the Grote Markt.
- `RAZE-1748-0489`, 5 October 1556: **Adriaen Jacopsen apteker** buys a house at the corner by the stadhuis.
- `RAZE-1748-0634` through `RAZE-1748-0637`, January 1558: **Adriaen Jacopsen Lampert** transfers multiple properties around the stadhuis/Grote Markt and in the Goese Polder.
- `RAZE-1748-0950` and `RAZE-1748-0951`, 1561: property transactions are made **ten laste van Adriaen Jacopsen Lampert**, including **Den Eenhoorn** against the stadhuis.

## Historical reconstruction

The identification **Adriaen Jacopsen apteker = Adriaen Jacopsen Lampert** is strongly supported by name, chronology and the overlapping stadhuis/Lange Vorststraat property environment, but no currently located act spells out the equation in one formula. Treat it as **SUPPORTED**, not literal archival proof.

The hypothesis that Adriaen is the son/successor of the older apotheker Jacob/Jacop Lampart/Lambert is also strongly plausible: `Jacopsen` is the expected patronymic, `Lampert` continues the name, and the `Den Eenhoorn` property links the older apothecary household to later obligations of Adriaen. No currently located act explicitly says “Adriaen, son of Jacob Lambert the apothecary.” Treat this historical kinship as **SUPPORTED/UNPROVEN**. The novel may adopt it as explicit fictional genealogy.

The transition `die Nyen Zwaene` (1553) → `verbrand huis genaamd de Zwaene` (1555) is directly documented for Adriaen Jacopsen Lampert. The most plausible event is the 18 May 1554 city fire, but the 1555 deed itself describes the burned state rather than dating the fire event. Use **strongly supported by chronology**, not deed-explicit event attribution.

## Daughter search

No currently searchable transport record identifies a daughter of Adriaen Jacopsen Lampert. That absence is not evidence that he had no daughter: transport registers only name women when property, marriage, inheritance or another juridical role brings them into an act. Weeskamer, testamentary and estate material remains the higher-value archival route for future verification.

The fictional daughter **Mayken Adriaensdr. Lampert** is therefore **not a discovered historical person**. Her name is deliberately selected from the attested direct Lampart environment (`Mayken huisvrouw Jacop Lampart`, 1543), while her daughtership to Adriaen is novel canon.

## Guardrails

1. Never present Mayken Adriaensdr. Lampert as a historically recovered daughter.
2. Never turn the likely Adriaen-apotheker/Lampert identity into an unqualified source fact.
3. Never state that Jacob Lambert's fatherhood of Adriaen is archival fact; it is a strong reconstruction and, if used in-story, explicit fiction.
4. Do not collapse the Merricken/Mayken forms without further evidence.
5. The Zwaene fire loss may give Mayken a historically grounded shared memory of the 1554 catastrophe, but her personal presence, household reaction and biography remain fiction.
