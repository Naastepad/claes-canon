# SRC-HIST-MILITARY-PAY-LOWCOUNTRIES-1567-1700-001

- **Source ID:** `SRC-HIST-MILITARY-PAY-LOWCOUNTRIES-1567-1700-001`
- **Source type:** academic military-economic synthesis
- **Author:** Etienne Rooms
- **Title:** `Bezoldiging, bevoorrading en inkwartiering van de koninklijke troepen in de Spaanse Nederlanden (1567-1700)`
- **Publication:** *Bijdragen en Mededelingen betreffende de Geschiedenis der Nederlanden* 118; consulted via DBNL
- **Verification date:** 2026-08-30

## Supported baseline

For the royal army in the Spanish Netherlands, Rooms gives the ordinary infantryman's nominal soldij as approximately **4–5 stuivers per day**. The military contract also placed material obligations on government: soldiers should be paid, be able to obtain food at normal prices, and receive lodging, fire and light.

The source simultaneously emphasizes a critical practical limit: payment and provisioning could be irregular. Soldier unrest and mutiny cannot therefore be modelled from nominal pay alone.

## Claes use

Use `4–5 stuivers/day` as a **military pay context anchor**, not as a universal wage, not as a Goese civilian day wage and not as proof that a named soldier actually received cash on time.

Military presence can impose costs on towns and households through lodging, service money, provisioning and delayed reimbursement. Exact local obligations require a place/date-specific source.

## Guardrails

- Keep nominal soldij distinct from money actually received.
- Keep royal, rebel and later States-army pay systems distinct.
- Do not compare a soldier's daily figure directly with a civilian day wage without accounting for workdays, rations, lodging and payment regularity.
- Exact unit/rank/nationality pay rates require a narrower source than this baseline.
