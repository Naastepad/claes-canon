## Relevant evidence

The article reconstructs sixteenth-century South-Beveland transport from period maps and archival references. It states that **Goes had a beurtveer on Antwerp**, as did Biezelinge. It also explains that both the Scheldt/Oosterschelde route and the Honte/Westerschelde were used for passenger and goods traffic, with the Honte gaining importance during the century. For the mid-1560s it regards Antwerp–Biezelinge beurtvaart as an ordinary route for visitors and goods.

## Project use

This is direct support for treating Antwerp as a routine reachable commercial node from Goes/South Beveland in Cornelis' lifetime. It removes the need to invent an exceptional private maritime route merely to connect Cornelis to Antwerp.

## Guardrails

1. A documented public/beurt connection does not prove which service Cornelis or De Hont used on a specific voyage.
2. Do not back-project later eighteenth-century beurtvaart regulations or monopolies into the 1550s–1560s.
3. The source establishes route existence and normality, not exact voyage duration or freight tariff.
