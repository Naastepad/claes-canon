## Critical provenance note

The IISG explicitly states that this is **not a dataset in the normal sense**. It is the surviving archive of the former Historical Prices and Wages website: spreadsheets, PDF files, tabular conversions and loose webpages. Provenance, place, unit, commodity definition and transformation history therefore have to be checked per file before values are compared or inserted into the Storybible.

The economic layer must never treat the container as one harmonized price series.

## Directly relevant Netherlands tabular files

- `netherlands-bread-prices-1594-1855.tab`: 14 variables, 299 observations.
- `netherlands-rye-wheat-prices-1594-1855.tab`: 17 variables, 283 observations.

Both overlap Claes' late life from **1594 through 1602**. They are high-value inputs for the late economic state, but only after variable metadata, quoted units, spatial scope and original provenance have been audited.

They do **not** supply a direct Goese 1540s–1570s price series.

## Required use rule

Before a numerical IISG value enters scene prose or a derived purchasing-power calculation, record source file, year, place/spatial coverage, commodity definition, original unit/currency, transformation status and original provenance where available.

## Prohibited shortcut

Do not convert an IISG series to a modern euro equivalent and present that as the meaning of historical money. Prefer historical purchasing-power anchors such as wage-days, bread/grain quantities, lodging, books, freight or other period goods, with proxy level explicitly marked.
