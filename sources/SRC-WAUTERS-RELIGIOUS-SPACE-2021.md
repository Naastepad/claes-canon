# SRC-WAUTERS-RELIGIOUS-SPACE-2021

**Type:** historical research / sensory history / religious space  
**Author:** Wendy Wauters  
**Primary work:** *De beroering van de religieuze ruimte. De belevingswereld van kerkgangers in de Antwerpse Onze-Lieve-Vrouwekerk, ca. 1450-1566* (doctoral dissertation, KU Leuven, 2021).  
**Companion/public-facing work consulted:** *De geuren van de kathedraal*.

## Scope
Wauters reconstructs the lived, sensory, social and material experience of the Antwerp Onze-Lieve-Vrouwekerk before the 1566 iconoclasm. The study explicitly uses the sensorium, psychogeography and a holistic reading of ritual, objects, architecture, guild and confraternity accounts, images and liturgical sources.

## What this source supports for Claes

### 1. Historical language and model of the senses
The source documents period concepts such as **die uterste sinne**, **den uutwendighen sinnen** and **die vijf sinnen des lichaems**. Sight, hearing, smell, taste and touch provide distinct sensory data; the *sensus communis* compares and orders them. The senses were also treated morally as gateways toward the inner person and therefore as faculties to be guarded and disciplined.

This provides historical support for treating *sinne* in the Claes project as embodied perception that can develop through sensation, comparison, pattern and interpretation. It does **not** prove that Claes personally studied Aristotelian faculty psychology unless separately established.

### 2. Church as sensory environment
The pre-Tridentine urban church was dynamic rather than silent or visually static. Multiple liturgical and non-liturgical activities could overlap. Sensory components include changing light, textiles, altar curtains, bells, chant, sermons, footsteps, crowd noise, incense, wax, flowers/herbs, bodies, dampness and proximity of graves and death.

The altar bell could create a temporary acoustic focus around the elevation; multiple devotions could produce bodily movement through the building. Sight included controlled visibility and concealment. Smell could work as atmosphere, purification, memory and association.

### 3. Church as social topography
The church interior functioned as a spatial expression of urban society. Choir access, altar enclosures, seating, processional position, burial locations and the prestige of locations made social status and group identity spatially legible. Movement through the church could communicate belonging.

### 4. Guilds, crafts, confraternities and other lay groups
Lay corporations were active makers of the religious space rather than passive users. Guilds, crafts and confraternities could maintain side altars, fund masses, commissions, textiles, lamps, candles, bells, music, flowers/herbs and processions. Corporate symbols and patron saints strengthened internal solidarity and public reputation. Wealth and status affected the sensory and material richness of an altar zone.

Antwerp-specific numbers, altar dedications, locations and levels of wealth must **not** be copied directly to Goes or Reimerswaal.

### 5. Rhetoricians / rederijkers
The study situates rhetoricians among the urban lay/corporate field and demonstrates intersections between rhetorical culture and religious themes. It cites the Landjuweel of 1496, where the urban government promoted a devotional theme connected to the Circumcision relic, and discusses *Elckerlijc*, performed by the Leuven chamber Die Kersauwe, where the five senses appear in a moral framework.

This supports treating rederijker culture as a vernacular medium through which religious, moral and sensory concepts could circulate. It does **not** support assuming that every rederijkerskamer owned an altar or held formal liturgical functions.

### 6. Church as collective and personal memory palace
Graves, inscriptions, donor portraits, heraldry, windows, retables, saints, endowed masses and donated objects embedded individuals and groups into the church. The building could therefore be experienced as a collective and personal **memory palace** in which the same physical zone carried different meanings for different people.

This is especially useful for Claes: an unchanged building may be experienced differently before and after fire, bereavement, displacement, confessional conflict or trauma.

### 7. Processions and city-church permeability
Objects, bells, relics, guilds, clergy and ritual moved across the boundary between church and city. Processions could turn streets into temporary extensions of sacred space. Church bells extended religious and civic order beyond visual range.

## Transferability guardrail
Wauters explicitly presents Antwerp as useful for thinking about sensory experience and ornamenta sacra in rich parish churches of urbanised regions of the Low Countries, while also stressing local variation. The Antwerp pre-Tridentine altar pattern resembles patterns found in urban parish churches in the Northern Netherlands, but no local pattern is identical.

Therefore use three evidence levels in Claes:

1. **ANTWERP_SOURCE_DIRECT** — documented for Antwerp and usable there as historical anchor.
2. **LOW_COUNTRIES_TRANSFERABLE** — practice or sensory mechanism sufficiently broad to use as an evidence-based reconstruction for Goes/Reimerswaal when scaled to local context.
3. **LOCAL_VERIFICATION_REQUIRED** — Antwerp-specific numbers, guild dedications, altar ownership, spatial positions, object inventories, feast calendars, processional routes, liturgical localisms and post-1566 chronology.

## Narrative consequence
The church should not be written merely as a devotional backdrop. It is simultaneously:
- liturgical institution;
- built environment;
- sensory field;
- social map;
- corporate stage;
- memory repository;
- acoustic and olfactory landmark in the city;
- interface between private memory and public identity.

For Claes, the historical model can be summarized as:

**body → sinne → spatial/social orientation → comparison/pattern → understanding → moral discernment**

This is historical scaffolding for the canonical *sinne* arc, not a claim that Wauters' model itself is Claes canon.
