# Pollmann — Memory in Early Modern Europe, 1500–1800

Judith Pollmann, *Memory in Early Modern Europe, 1500–1800* (Oxford University Press, 2017).

Research use for Claes:
- early modern memory operated through intertwined oral, written, visual, performative, spatial and material media;
- personal and public memory influenced one another and changed over time;
- streets, fields, buildings, inscriptions, objects, plays, songs and rituals could carry local memory;
- memories of extreme violence could remain unspoken, especially while danger and survival pressures continued; shame, guilt and pain could inhibit narration;
- atrocity narratives could shape later memories of violence;
- after religious and political rupture, communities could construct new narratives of continuity.

Narrative relevance: these findings support the Claes architecture in which fire, flood, war, objects, songs, religious spaces and repeated sensory encounters become carriers of memory without requiring modern psychological vocabulary in-world. They support a distinction between event, personal recollection, public story and later cultural memory.

Guardrail: Pollmann provides comparative and Low Countries synthesis. Do not use this source alone to assert a specific personal trauma response for Claes or exact local Goes/Reimerswaal commemorative practice.
