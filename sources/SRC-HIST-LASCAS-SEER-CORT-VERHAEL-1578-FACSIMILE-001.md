## Exact observations used for Claes canon

The consulted copy shows:

- the visible title page on PDF page 9;
- a separate page headed *Den oversetter aenden Leser* on PDF page 11;
- Las Casas’ prologue addressed to Prince Philip beginning on PDF page 12 and ending on PDF page 15;
- the proper tract, headed *Cort verhael vande Destructie van Indien*, beginning on PDF page 16;
- continuous Las Casas text through PDF page 147, followed by a blank page 148 in the consulted scan;
- a visible `A2` signature at the foot of PDF page 17, confirming ordinary gathering/signature practice in the visible 1578 book.

For the Claes project, the contiguous Las Casas body from PDF pages **12 through 147 inclusive** comprises **136 scanned page surfaces**. At eight pages per single-sheet quarto gathering, that supplies an exact **17-sheet capacity analogue** for the fictional hidden memoriaal carrier.

The first eight page surfaces of that body (PDF pp. 12–19) provide the project’s sheet-A content sequence: prologue to Philip; end of prologue; opening of *Cort verhael*; description of the Indians; sheep versus wolves/tigers/lions; geographic scale of destruction; principal modes of destruction; and gold/wealth as motive.

## Claims supported

This facsimile supports:

- the sequence and compactness of the later Brabant/Dutch Las Casas text;
- the distinction between visible-edition paratext (title page and translator’s address) and Las Casas’ own prologue/text;
- use of ordinary signatures/gathering control in the visible edition;
- the project inference that the Las Casas body can serve as a 136-page / 17-quarto-sheet capacity model.

## Reliability / limitations

This source does **not** prove that a hidden 1564 Brabant/Dutch edition existed, that Silvius printed such an edition, or that tannin/gum invisible relief printing was historically used. It is a later textual and typographic witness used as an empirical capacity and sequence analogue for novel canon.

The exact line endings, page breaks and visual design of the fictional 1564 hidden edition are not inherited from 1578. In particular, the 1578 title typography, ornaments and decorated initials are not canonical visual templates for the memoriaal.
