## Stadtimmerman target

The compilation identifies **Anthonis Claesz / Anthonis Claiszoon** as a Goese **stadtimmerman** in weeskamer-related material and traces him in that role/family context in 1567, 1580 and 1590. The same discussion points researchers to the Goese weeskamer notulenboeken.

This makes Anthonis Claesz a high-value **name target** for the pre-1602 municipal-account extraction: city carpenter payments, public-works warrants, acquittances, retainer/salary entries or day-work entries may be indexed or recognisable by his name.

### Wage evidence boundary

The consulted compilation does **not** provide a day-wage figure for Anthonis Claesz. His title `stadtimmerman` does not establish whether he was paid by annual retainer, day rate, piecework, contract, materials-plus-labour, or a mixed arrangement. No numeric wage may be inferred from the office title alone.

## Goese bierdragers — 1591

The compilation transcribes `RAZE 2039, fol. 219, 7-5-1591`, naming two Goese poorters with beer-carrying occupations:

- Cornelis Jacobsen Schipper, about 30 years old: **bierdrager en poorter**;
- Adriaen Cornelis Faes, about 29 years old: **schipper en bierdrager en poorter**.

This is direct local evidence that beer carrying was an identifiable occupation in Goes in 1591. It does **not** establish the carrier tariff, a daily wage, monopoly/exclusivity, headcount or the precise guild rules then in force.

## South-Beveland beer impost and biersteker — 1593

A March 1593 attestation concerns Jan Jacobsz Bom as **impostmeester van de bieren** for the quarter of 's-Heer Hendrikskinderen, Wissenkerke and 's-Heer Arendskerke for the half-year ending at the end of March 1593. The same evidence names Michiel Horen as **biersteker** and states that Cornelis Dignusz obtained five tons of `dobbelbier` for a wedding through him.

This is useful evidence for a South-Beveland distribution/tax mechanism close to Goes: beer could pass through a named biersteker while beer impost administration operated at quarter level. It is **not** proof of a city-Goes impost rate and does not establish that every biersteker was the legal taxpayer.

## Goese rye prices across 1595 — corrected chronology

The direct transcription of `RAZE 2040, fol. 109` records a late-October/early-November 1595 attestation about **earlier transactions during the summer of 1595**. Lowijs Serwouters Jacobsz, grain buyer in Goes, checked his book and recalled:

- **April 1595:** one last `oosterschen rogghe` bought in Goes from Pieter Crijnsz, schipper, at **£26 Vlaams per last**;
- **May 1595:** two hoed rye from Pieter de Windt at **14 schellingen 2 groten per sack**, equivalent in the attestation to **£26 11 schellingen 3 groten per last**;
- **around 17–18 August 1595:** one last rye from Adriaen Cornelisz de Doodt of Alkmaar at **£19 Vlaams per last**.

Pieter Jansz van Oosten, about 50, poorter, schepen and grain buyer of Goes, independently attested that he had bought at the same prices from the same sellers.

### Dating / locator discrepancy

The compilation labels the direct record around `31 October / 1 November 1595`; its primary locator is **RAZE 2040, fol. 109**. Boonman's later metrological synthesis reports the 14s2g figure as a 31 October transaction and cites fol. 110. The direct Levendale transcription shows that **14s2g per sack is the May transaction remembered in the later attestation**, not a 31 October spot purchase.

Project use therefore follows the transaction chronology in the direct transcription and flags the Boonman folio/date presentation as a source discrepancy rather than silently harmonising it.

## Goese credit / debt — 1585

A Goese notarial record of 14 June 1585 has Jan Pauwelsz Cuijper acknowledge a debt of **17 pond groten Vlaams** to Franchois Hertsinck for accumulated `verteringen` in Hertsinck's inn, binding his person and goods as security.

The following day, related material concerns Herman Piersz of Nisse, who had farmed imposten for the Nisse quarter and owed a much larger balance to the receiver-general; seizure and surety are part of the mechanism described.

This is strong local evidence that ordinary commerce and hospitality operated through **book debt, acknowledgement, security, surety and enforcement**, not only through immediate cash payment.

**Boundary:** the 17-pound sum is accumulated debt, not the price of one meal, one night or one tavern visit.

## Additional local construction evidence

The same Levendale compilation contains multiple late-sixteenth-century Goese construction transactions, including:

- named Goese timmerlieden and metselaars in valuation and building disputes;
- a house-building dispute in which a metselaar undertook materials plus labour and an arbitration fixed a lump sum;
- the 1582 brewery valuation by Goese building craftsmen.

These demonstrate active local craft/contract practice but are **contract/valuation evidence**, not day-wage observations unless labour time and rate are explicitly stated.

## Guardrails

1. Use the compilation to locate names, dates and primary record families.
2. For an `A_DIRECT` wage, return to the underlying dated account/folio whenever possible.
3. Do not convert a lump-sum contract or valuation into a daily wage.
4. Keep `stadtimmerman` remuneration form OPEN until the payment record says otherwise.
5. `Bierdrager` proves an occupation, not a known tariff or day wage.
6. Keep the 1593 quarter-level South-Beveland beer-impost evidence distinct from a city-Goes tax rule.
7. Treat the 1595 rye evidence as an April/May/August price sequence attested later in the year; never relabel the May 14s2g price as an October spot price.
8. A debt acknowledgement is not a retail price list.
