# SRC-HIST-SMALLEGANGE-NIEUWE-CRONYK-ZEELAND-1696-001

## Identification

- **Type:** early modern chronicle / regional historical compilation
- **Author / compiler:** Mattheus Smallegange, building on the Reygersbergh/Boxhorn chronicle tradition
- **Title:** *Nieuwe Cronyk van Zeeland*
- **Date:** 1696 edition, bibliographic details to be verified against the exact consulted copy
- **Consulted form:** conversation upload `Nieuwe_Cronyk_van_Zeeland(2).pdf`, August 2026 working extraction

## Use in Claes project

This source is valuable for:

- Zeeland-centric framing of history and memory;
- civic and institutional self-description;
- Goes, Tholen, Middelburg, Arnemuiden and Delta-waterway passages;
- later retrospective language around satisfactie, Reformation, public Catholic worship and civic government;
- lists of Zeeuwse learned men, including medical, poetic, legal and chymical figures.

## Source-status guardrails

This is a **later chronicle**, not a contemporary diary of Claes' lifetime. It may preserve older material and municipal traditions, but its language and emphases are also seventeenth-century memory work. For scene use, distinguish:

1. event reported;
2. wording and interpretation in the later chronicle;
3. what a sixteenth-century inhabitant could plausibly perceive;
4. fictional use for Claes.

Do not treat every Smallegange/Reygersbergh statement as direct contemporary evidence without corroboration when local microhistory or exact chronology matters.

## Claims currently extracted for authoring use

- Goes and Tholen are described retrospectively as having received satisfactie agreements from Prince William that tolerated the Catholic religion with public worship; the later account says both cities gradually accepted the Reformation and requested that the satisfactie articles be annulled.
- Johan Reygersberg of Cortgene is reported as author of the Zeeland chronicle, printed at Antwerp in 1551.
- Theobaldus ab Hogelande of Middelburg is reported as a learned philosopher and chymicus, associated with the *Theatrum Chymicum*.
- Livinus Lemnius of Zierikzee and Cornelis van Baersdorp of Goes provide Zeeuwse medical/reputational background.
- Arnemuiden/Middelburg passages support a commercial-harbour and regulatory environment but do not by themselves prove any specific Claes encounter.
