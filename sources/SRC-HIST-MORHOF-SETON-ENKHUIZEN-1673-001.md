# SRC-HIST-MORHOF-SETON-ENKHUIZEN-1673-001 — Morhof's retrospective Seton tradition

**Type:** retrospective seventeenth-century historical report  
**Author:** Daniel Georg Morhof  
**Work:** *De Metallorum Transmutatione*  
**Date:** 1673  
**Scope used here:** Alexander Seton/Sidonius and the reported Enkhuizen projection of 1602.

## Evidence used

Morhof reports that in Amsterdam he was shown a small golden plate said to derive from gold made by the Scotsman **Alexander Seton (Sidonius)** from lead at **Enkhuizen**. He gives a chain of custody through the family of physician Johannes Antonides van der Linden and says the fragment originally came from the sailor **Jacob Hausfsen**, in whose house Seton had performed the projection.

Morhof further reports that the Enkhuizen physician had recorded the circumstances in his own hand as:

- year: **1602**;
- date: **13 March**;
- time: **the fourth hour after noon**.

For the novel this supplies the chosen historical frame for the 1602 projectio scene.

## Source-status guardrail

Morhof writes about seventy years after the reported event. His account is therefore **retrospective transmitted testimony**, not a contemporaneous eyewitness record available here. The novel may adopt its concrete frame while preserving that evidential distance.

## What this source does not prove

- It does not prove literal lead-to-gold transmutation.
- It does not document a cupellation-like mechanism.
- It does not provide the composition of Seton's powder.
- It does not settle every witness, furnace step, quantity, location detail within the house or immediate aftermath.
