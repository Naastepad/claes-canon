# SRC-HIST-GOES-SCHUTTERIJ-DEGHEYN-001 — civic shooting guilds and later drill codification

**Type:** historical military/civic-practice source bundle  
**Status:** LOCAL_GOES_ANCHORS_PLUS_LATE_COMPARATOR  
**Scope:** Goese schuttersgilden in Claes' lifetime and Jacob de Gheyn's 1607 *Wapenhandelinghe* as a non-retrojectable later comparator.

## Goese civic shooting culture

Local Goese heritage sources agree on a long premodern shooting-guild tradition:

- **Sint Joris / Edele Voetboog**: crossbow tradition predating Claes; locally associated with the devies **`Van Ongenugten Vrij`**;
- **Sint Sebastiaan**: handbow guild attested around/from 1469, devies `Altijd in Roeren` in the local heritage synthesis;
- a firearms/kolveniers guild under Sint Adriaan / Edele Busse existed in the first half of the sixteenth century.

The exact start date of the firearm guild is **not currently normalized**: Zeeuwse Ankers gives 1516, while an Erfgoed van Goes archival introduction gives 1530. Until the underlying archive/provenance is reconciled, the safe statement is `present by the first half of the sixteenth century`; neither 1516 nor 1530 should be treated as unquestioned story fact.

The guilds combine defence, weapon practice, competition and sociability/status. **Papegaai shooting is locally supported**: competitors shoot at an artificial bird on a high pole, and the successful shooter can be recognized as **schutterskoning**. This creates a historically grounded public performance of weapon skill and recognition without implying that every guild session was a king-shooting event.

## Local topography of the bow guilds

Erfgoed van Goes places the exercise grounds/hoven of Sint Joris and Sint Sebastiaan beside one another on the **south-west side of Goes, within the walls and south of the church**, oriented in modern terms to the later Kreukelmarkt / Van de Spiegelstraat / Wijngaardstraat area.

For sixteenth-century prose:

- use the spatial relation to church, wall and neighbouring guild grounds;
- treat modern street names as research orientation only unless period naming is separately established;
- this proximity means a child moving through central/southern Goes can plausibly experience the schuttershoven as an ordinary civic place rather than a remote military compound.

Later nineteenth-century antiquarian descriptions also preserve the tradition that the handbow and footbow hoven lay south of the church. Those later descriptions are corroborative topographic memory, not direct evidence for every mid-sixteenth-century building detail.

## Papegaai / schutterskoning — scene boundary

The local sources support the practice and title, not a fictional outcome for Cornelis.

Safe uses:

- Cornelis competes or attends within his fictional Sint-Joris membership;
- Claes observes the difference between private practice and public judged skill;
- a historically plausible winner receives communal recognition as schutterskoning;
- the event can provide crowd, waiting, judging, missed shots, impact, sociability and status texture.

Not established by the historical source bundle:

- Cornelis winning;
- an exact year/date in Claes' childhood;
- the exact prize, chain, silver bird or ceremonial sequence for mid-sixteenth-century Goes;
- exact rules, distances or order of shooting.

Those require separate evidence or explicit story decisions.

## Distinctions required for 1572 scenes

Keep separate:

1. **schuttersgilde** — corporate shooting/defence/social institution;
2. **broader civic watch / weerbare inhabitants** — townsmen mobilised for defence;
3. **garrison/professional soldiers** — military personnel under command structures;
4. **later standardized Mauritian drill** — increasingly formalized military practice of the later war.

Evidence for seventeenth-century Goese vendels, clothing and musket equipment is not automatically evidence for 1572.

## Jacob de Gheyn — 1607 comparator

Jacob de Gheyn II's *Wapenhandelinghe van roers, musquetten ende spiessen* was published in 1607. Rijksmuseum documentation describes 117 engravings showing roerschutters, musketeers and pikemen and depicts weapon actions step by step, from loading/handling to firing or pike use.

For the Claes project its correct role is:

- a **late visual/technical comparator** for the mature standardization of drill around the Mauritian military reforms;
- useful for late-world texture and for understanding how individual weapon handling can be decomposed into ordered actions;
- **not** a manual that Goese defenders in 1572 are assumed to know or follow.

Likewise, details from Goese schutter pieces of 1616–1665 — musket fork, `twelve apostles`, costume, exact fire-rate claims and later vendel organization — must not be silently projected back to Claes' 1572 environment.

## Writing guardrails

1. Keep **Sint Joris = voetboog** and **Sint Sebastiaan = handboog** in current project canon.
2. `Van Ongenugten Vrij` is historically attached to the Sint-Joris/voetboog tradition; do not invent a specific banner, lintel or inscription location without support.
3. Papegaai shooting and the title schutterskoning are supported; Cornelis winning is not.
4. Use the south-of-church / south-western guild-ground topography, but do not project modern street names into sixteenth-century speech.
5. Record the 1516/1530 firearm-guild date conflict; do not silently choose one.
6. No De Gheyn 1607 drill as 1572 eyewitness procedure.
7. No automatic 1616/1624 `twelve apostles`, musket fork, clothing or exact rate-of-fire in 1572.
8. Distinguish guild member, civic defender and professional soldier.
9. Weapon practice is bodily sequence, attention and coordination; precise commands/formations require contemporaneous support.
10. Cornelis' Sint-Joris membership is created only by explicit story decision; no historical Cornelis office follows from this source bundle.
