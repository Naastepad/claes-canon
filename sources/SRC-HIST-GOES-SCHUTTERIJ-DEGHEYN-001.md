# SRC-HIST-GOES-SCHUTTERIJ-DEGHEYN-001 — civic shooting guilds and later drill codification

**Type:** historical military/civic-practice source bundle  
**Status:** LOCAL_GOES_ANCHORS_PLUS_LATE_COMPARATOR  
**Scope:** Goese schuttersgilden in Claes' lifetime and Jacob de Gheyn's 1607 *Wapenhandelinghe* as a non-retrojectable later comparator.

## Goese civic shooting culture

Local Goese heritage sources agree on a long premodern shooting-guild tradition:

- Sint Joris / Edele Voetboog: crossbow tradition predating Claes;
- Sint Sebastiaan: handbow guild attested around/from 1469;
- a firearms/kolveniers guild under Sint Adriaan / Edele Busse existed in the first half of the sixteenth century.

The exact start date of the firearm guild is **not currently normalized**: Zeeuwse Ankers gives 1516, while an Erfgoed van Goes archival introduction gives 1530. Until the underlying archive/provenance is reconciled, the safe statement is `present by the first half of the sixteenth century`; neither 1516 nor 1530 should be treated as unquestioned story fact.

The guilds combine defence, weapon practice, competition and sociability/status. Papegaai shooting is locally supported as a competition form. Do not collapse these guilds into a modern standing army.

## Distinctions required for 1572 scenes

Keep separate:

1. **schuttersgilde** — corporate shooting/defence/social institution;
2. **broader civic watch / weerbare inhabitants** — townsmen mobilised for defence;
3. **garrison/professional soldiers** — military personnel under command structures;
4. **later standardized Mauritian drill** — increasingly formalized military practice of the later war.

Evidence for seventeenth-century Goese vendels, clothing and musket equipment is not automatically evidence for 1572.

## Jacob de Gheyn — 1607 comparator

Jacob de Gheyn II's *Wapenhandelinghe van roers, musquetten ende spiessen* was published in 1607. Rijksmuseum documentation describes 117 engravings showing roerschutters, musketeers and pikemen and depicts weapon actions step by step, from loading/handling to firing or pike use.

For the Claes project its correct role is:

- a **late visual/technical comparator** for the mature standardization of drill around the Mauritian military reforms;
- useful for late-world texture and for understanding how individual weapon handling can be decomposed into ordered actions;
- **not** a manual that Goese defenders in 1572 are assumed to know or follow.

Likewise, details from Goese schutter pieces of 1616–1665 — musket fork, `twelve apostles`, costume, exact fire-rate claims and later vendel organization — must not be silently projected back to Claes' 1572 environment.

## Writing guardrails

1. Record the 1516/1530 firearm-guild date conflict; do not silently choose one.
2. No De Gheyn 1607 drill as 1572 eyewitness procedure.
3. No automatic 1616/1624 `twelve apostles`, musket fork, clothing or exact rate-of-fire in 1572.
4. Distinguish guild member, civic defender and professional soldier.
5. Weapon practice is bodily sequence, attention and coordination; precise commands/formations require contemporaneous support.
6. Cornelis' membership or office in a shooting guild is not created by this dossier.
