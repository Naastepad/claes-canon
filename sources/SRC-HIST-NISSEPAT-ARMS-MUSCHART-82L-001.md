# SRC-HIST-NISSEPAT-ARMS-MUSCHART-82L-001 — Nissepat family arms / voetboog

**Type:** heraldic source/provenance record  
**Status:** CALLBACK_RECOVERED — DIRECT_SCAN_RECHECK_PENDING  
**Scope:** the Nissepat heraldic charge recorded in the CBG/Muschart collection and its use as historical substrate for novel canon.

## Recovered project record

Earlier project research identified the CBG/Muschart record:

- archive/object identifier: `NL-HaCBG_1801_0082_0819`;
- family name: `Nissepat`;
- Muschart classification/reference: `82L`;
- indexed bow terms recovered in the project: `boog`, `handboog`, `voetboog`;
- earlier project transcription/review of the descriptive line: **`een voetboog met den langen zwengel links`**.

The project explicitly reaffirmed on **18 July 2025** that the intended reading of the heraldic charge is **voetboog / crossbow**, not an ordinary handboog.

## Evidence boundary

The recovered record is stronger than the later shorthand that treated the heraldic image as wholly unreadable between handboog and voetboog. The earlier transcription contains the specific term `voetboog` and a `lange zwengel`, which is consistent with a mechanically spanned crossbow representation.

However, the following remain unverified in the present repository session and must not be promoted beyond their current status until the Muschart card is directly re-opened:

- the exact underlying historical source cited by Muschart;
- the exact original armiger;
- the callback association with **Jacob Jansz./Janz. Nissepat**;
- the callback date of approximately **1581**;
- whether the underlying object was specifically a surviving seal attached to a charter;
- the exact historical spanning mechanism represented by `zwengel`.

Therefore the project may treat **the Nissepat charge as a voetboog** as the recovered heraldic reading, while keeping the armiger/date/charter chain explicitly pending direct provenance recheck.

## CBG/Muschart method note

The CBG describes Muschart cards as heraldic working records that normally include a family/person name, a description of the arms and a reference to the source used by Muschart. For this project the next provenance step is therefore to recover the source line on card `NL-HaCBG_1801_0082_0819` itself rather than infer a primary document from later secondary references.

CBG collection information: https://cbg.nl/bronnen/collectie-muschart/

## Related local historical institution

The separate Goese schutterij source bundle `SRC-HIST-GOES-SCHUTTERIJ-DEGHEYN-001` establishes the local institutional distinction used by the novel:

- **Sint Joris / Edele Voetboog** — crossbow/voetboog tradition;
- **Sint Sebastiaan** — handbow tradition;
- **Sint Adriaan / Edele Busse** — firearm/kolvenier tradition.

This local institutional fact does **not** prove that a historical Nissepat armiger or fictional Cornelis belonged to Sint Joris. The bridge from family heraldry to Cornelis' personal membership is deliberate story canon.

## Writing/provenance guardrails

1. Do not revert the project to `handboog or voetboog equally unreadable` unless direct inspection disproves the recovered transcription.
2. Do not present Jacob Jansz. Nissepat, ca. 1581, or a specific charter/seal as verified until Muschart's source line is directly recovered.
3. Do not infer that every Nissepat was a crossbowman because the family arms contain a voetboog.
4. Do not infer Cornelis' historical existence or guild membership from the arms.
5. Do not identify `lange zwengel` with one exact cranequin/windlass subtype without separate evidence.
6. Keep Sint Joris/voetboog, Sint Sebastiaan/handboog and Sint Adriaan/firearm traditions distinct.
