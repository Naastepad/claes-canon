# SRC-SECONDARY-DAMME-WATERRECHT-MARITIME-RISK-001 — Waterrecht / zeerecht of Damme

- **Source ID:** `SRC-SECONDARY-DAMME-WATERRECHT-MARITIME-RISK-001`
- **Source type:** historical legal tradition, consulted through a modern explanatory transcription/translation
- **Coverage:** medieval-to-early-modern maritime custom; the combined tradition was printed in the sixteenth century as the Gotland/Wisby waterrecht
- **Consulted page:** https://heykenniscentrum.be/geschiedenis/heemkundige-teksten/117471-het-zeerecht-van-damme-1996-02
- **Verification date:** 2026-08-30
- **Use in this project:** general responsibility/risk structure for merchant freight scenes, not a claim that every clause applied verbatim in Goes or Delft in every year

## Relevant rules

The transmitted rules distinguish ordinary commercial cargo risk from damage caused by the shipper/master's own failures.

Examples relevant to Cornelis / De Hont authoring:

- the master is responsible if he sails contrary to prudent advice and cargo is lost as a result;
- the master must use suitable mooring, tackle and handling arrangements;
- damage caused by bad loading/stowage or negligent securing of casks can be charged to the master/crew;
- if storm damage occurs despite proper securing and the master/crew can establish absence of negligence, liability does not automatically fall on the master;
- cargo deliberately jettisoned to save ship and remaining goods is treated as a shared/general-average type loss;
- extraordinary sacrifices such as cutting mast or anchor may likewise be shared;
- salvaged cargo can still generate freight obligations.

## Authoring implication

Do not write a modern binary rule such as `all risk passes to the schipper once loaded` or `Cornelis always bears every loss`.

Preferred model:

`commercial/inherent/market risk -> normally with merchant/cargo interest`

`negligent stowage/handling/navigation -> potentially with schipper/master`

`genuine common peril / jettison / extraordinary sacrifice -> shared according to maritime custom`

## Boundary

This source represents a broad maritime legal tradition. It is appropriate for mechanism and scene logic, but not for inventing an exact local Goese clause, insurance percentage, freight rate, or court outcome without a local contract or judgment.
