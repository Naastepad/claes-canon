## Relevant account entry

Under `Ander uijtgeeff van scheepvracht ende diergelijcke in den jaere 1572 gedaen`, the accounts state that, in order to carry **Barthel Entes with troops from Holland toward Goes**, various vessels lying before Delft and at **Delfshaven** were arrested/requisitioned. The account then records **21 vessels** receiving an allowance of 42 guilders each, with adjustments for sums already received in Zeeland.

Elsewhere in the same account context Barthel Entes' movement toward Zuid-Beveland is provisioned with beer and other supplies.

## What this supports

- A functioning **Delft / Delfshaven -> Zeeland / Goes operational shipping corridor** is directly evidenced in 1572.
- Delft's maritime access through Delfshaven was not merely theoretical infrastructure; multiple vessels could be mobilised for the Goes theatre.
- For the novel, a Delft-based schipper participating in ordinary Holland–Zeeland movement is therefore geographically and operationally plausible.

## Guardrails

1. This is **military requisition**, not a recovered commercial beer contract.
2. The entry supplies no Delft–Goes transit duration for ordinary freight.
3. It does not name Aernouldt de Hont and does not prove he was active in 1572.
4. It does not establish the vessel types, capacities or normal freight rates of the 21 ships.
5. Use it to strengthen corridor plausibility, not to back-fill De Hont's biography.
