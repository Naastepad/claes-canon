# SRC-LEX-MNW-BIERSTEKER-BIERWERKER-16C-001 — sixteenth-century Zeeland beer-trade terminology

- **Source ID:** `SRC-LEX-MNW-BIERSTEKER-BIERWERKER-16C-001`
- **Source type:** historical lexicographical synthesis citing sixteenth-century legal/civic records
- **Work:** *Middelnederlandsch Handwoordenboek — Supplement*, entries under `bier-`
- **Consulted representation:** IVdNT digital PDF/search result, verified 2026-08-30
- **Geographic value:** especially high because the cited evidence includes **Middelburg** in the fifteenth and first half of the sixteenth century

## Biersteken / biersteker

The supplement gives:

- `biersteken`: **van buiten ingevoerde bieren verkopen**;
- `biersteker / biersteecker`: **hij die tappers voorziet van van buiten ingevoerde bieren**.

The entry cites, among other records:

- Middelburg, 1459;
- Middelburg, first half of the sixteenth century;
- North-Holland city law around 1530;
- Brielle 1548-1549.

### Project consequence

For sixteenth-century Zeeland, the strongest period-near lexical sense is not `beer blender` but **commercial supplier/distributor of imported beer to tappers**.

This does not mean every biersteker handled only foreign beer in every later locality, but it provides the safest role baseline for Cornelis:

`outside brewer / imported beer -> biersteker -> tapper / retail outlet`

The verb `steken` in this occupational term must **not** be interpreted automatically as blending/mixing beer. Any blending practice requires separate evidence.

## Bierwerken / bierwerker

The same supplement gives:

- `bierwercken`: **bier lossen, vervoeren**;
- `bierwercker / bierwerker`: one who **unloads beer from ships or transports beer**;
- `bierwerckersghilde`: guild of beer workers, with a **Middelburg 1545** reference.

This establishes a period-correct distinction between beer trading/distribution and the heavy physical unloading/transport function.

### Goes boundary

The lexical evidence proves a sixteenth-century Zeeland/Middelburg beer-worker institution, not a specific pre-1600 Goese monopoly or guild name. Goes has separate direct evidence for named `bierdragers` in 1591; that evidence must be used for Goes itself.

## Occupational-overlap boundary

Historical occupational labels need not be mutually exclusive. The Goese 1591 evidence includes Adriaen Cornelis Faes as both **schipper and bierdrager**. Therefore the project must not invent a rule that a bierdrager, schipper and biersteker could never overlap as persons or investments.

Likewise, no current sixteenth-century Goese source proves that a biersteker was legally forbidden to own a vessel. Whether Cornelis owns, co-owns, charters, books freight with, or simply receives from a schipper remains a separate authorial/evidence question.

## Guardrails

1. `biersteker` ≠ brewer by default.
2. `biersteker` ≠ beer blender by etymological guess.
3. Safest sixteenth-century Zeeland sense: supplier of imported beer to tappers.
4. `bierwerker/bierdrager` = physical unloading/transport function; exact Goese institutional rules require local evidence.
5. Do not project the later Goese `Nassaugilde` name backwards into the sixteenth century without a dated source.
6. Do not state that bierstekers were forbidden to own ships unless a local ordinance proves it.
