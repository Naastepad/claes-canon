## Governing problem

`GAP.ECON.GOES.WAGES.1540_1602` cannot be solved by substituting Antwerp, Bergen op Zoom, Holland or a western-Netherlands index for Goes. The required evidence is a dated **Goese** observation that identifies the work/occupation, amount, historical money unit and, where relevant, in-kind payment.

The De Vries source pass established that he used Goese municipal material, but an independent inventory-number/date cross-check shows that his cited range `783-959` cannot be assumed to cover Claes' lifetime: `Stad Goes, inv.nr. 784` is independently cited for a 1627 event. The route below therefore starts again from demonstrably pre-1602 Goese source families.

## 1. Official city archive — authority and administrative source architecture

Official archive:

- **Archive:** Stad Goes, `(1267)1449-1851(1927)`
- **Access / reference:** `NL-GsGA-1.1-001`
- **Archive code:** `001`
- **Repository:** Gemeentearchief Goes / GOES Publiek
- **Public access:** public archive description/inventory

The official archive description states that from **1438** city finances were administered by **two annually elected rentmeesters**. One performed the financial administration and the other supervised. Public property and public works were overseen by three stadsdirecteuren, with a **stadsfabriek** responsible for day-to-day execution.

### Research consequence

For a Claes-period civil wage series, the best local administrative search is therefore not a vague request for `the city accounts`, but a targeted search across:

1. rentmeester / city-finance accounts;
2. stadsfabriek / public-works expenditure;
3. project-specific accounts, warrants, acquittances or contractor payments;
4. guild accounts where labour, fees, repair work or hired assistance is itemised.

The existence of the administrative structure does **not** prove that every year or every occupational wage survives.

## 2. Pre-1602 guild source families

A Goese historical inventory of surviving guild material records several account series beginning inside Claes' lifetime.

Especially relevant:

- **timmerliedengilde:** account books from **1547-1798**; resolutions from **1586-1798**;
- the guild covered timmerlieden and, in the broader craft grouping described in Goese sources, related building/craft occupations including metselaars, kuipers, wagenmakers, stoeldraaiers, mandenmakers and others;
- **kramersgilde:** minutes from 1544, accounts from 1546;
- **schoen-/gareelmakers:** account books from 1546;
- **wevers/hoedenmakers:** account books from 1547;
- **kleermakers:** account books from 1555;
- **panneluidengilde:** resolutions survive from 1523 onward.

### Evidence boundary

A guild account book is **not automatically a wage book**. Guild accounts can contain entrance fees, annual contributions, fines, meals, property expenditure, repairs, ceremonial costs, loans and other transactions. A specific `loon`, `daggeld`, hired-work or named-worker entry must be read before a wage observation exists.

The timmerliedengilde series is nevertheless a high-value local source because:

- it starts in 1547, directly inside Claes' childhood;
- it concerns occupations likely to appear in city repair/building expenditure;
- it offers a second local evidence stream against which municipal public-works payments can be checked.

## 3. Additional pre-1572 municipal inventory leads

Published source repertories point to demonstrably sixteenth-century Goes material including:

- inv.nr. **188** — city-government incoming/outgoing documents, 1532-1549;
- inv.nr. **189** — 1551-1565;
- inv.nr. **190** — 1566-1569;
- inv.nr. **191** — 1570-1571;
- inv.nr. **676** — Voorbodenboek, 1469-1564;
- inv.nr. **685** — copies/publications, including material from the sixteenth century;
- other low-numbered ASG/Stad Goes records are demonstrably pre-1602, so inventory numbering is not safely chronological.

These are not labelled wage series. They are useful for locating ordinances, public works, hiring rules, prices, taxes and administrative vocabulary that can lead to the actual payment records.

## 4. Search lexicon for direct extraction

Search/transcription should preserve original wording and include variants. Priority terms:

- `loon`, `loonen`, `werkloon`, `arbeidsloon`;
- `daggeld`, `dachgelt`, `dagloon`, `dachloon`;
- `arbeider`, `arbeiders`, `werckman`, `wercklieden`;
- `timmerman`, `timmerlieden`, `timmerknecht`;
- `metselaer`, `metselaar`, `metselaars`;
- `opperman`, `opperlieden`;
- `knecht`, `knechten`;
- `graver`, `gravers`;
- `drager`, `dragers`, `zakkedrager`, `bierdrager`;
- `pannelieden` / zoutarbeid;
- `stadsfabriek`, `fabriek`, `rentmeester`;
- `betaelt`, `uitbetaelt`, `gegeven`, `voor ... dagen`;
- task vocabulary for walls, gates, quay, harbour, bridges, paving, fire repair and fortification.

## 5. Minimum schema for a promotable Goese wage observation

Before a number becomes `A_DIRECT` or `B_LOCAL_SERIES`, record:

- exact date or account year;
- archive/access/inventory/folio;
- occupation or named worker;
- task/work context;
- number of days, if stated;
- cash amount and historical unit;
- whether the amount is per day, piece, contract, week or total;
- in-kind food/drink/lodging allowance, if any;
- whether master/journeyman/helper status is identifiable;
- raw transcription before conversion;
- derived stuiver/day calculation separately labelled as DERIVED.

A total contract sum with no labour-days is not a daily wage. A piece rate is not a daily wage. A guild fee is not a wage.

## 6. Current regional comparator — calibration only

Until direct Goes values are recovered, De Vries' 1550-1554 observations remain comparison material only:

- Bergen op Zoom: skilled construction labour **5.5-6 stuivers/day**, unskilled **3-4**;
- Antwerp: skilled **9 stuivers/day**, unskilled **4**.

These figures demonstrate that same-period city rates differ materially. They may reject implausible reconstructions but may never be averaged into a synthetic `Goes = X` value.

## 7. Current research decision

The local wage gap remains open:

`GAP.ECON.GOES.WAGES.1540_1602 = OPEN / HIGH`

The preferred extraction path is now:

`official archive inventory -> dated pre-1602 rentmeester/stadsfabriek records -> direct labour-payment entries -> guild cross-check -> normalized local anchors`

Not:

`regional wage -> assumed Goes wage`.

## Guardrails

1. Archive-series existence does not equal wage observation.
2. Guild-account existence does not equal daily-wage series.
3. Inventory number does not equal chronology; verify date coverage.
4. No regional comparator becomes a local Goes value.
5. Do not silently combine master, journeyman, helper and unskilled labour.
6. Do not convert a piece rate, contract total or guild fee into day wage without explicit labour-time evidence.
7. Do not use this dossier to close any fictional property/departure decision.
