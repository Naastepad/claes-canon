# SRC-HIST-GOES-FIRE-1554-REFINEMENT-001

## Type
Historical evidence and reconstruction-method supplement for the Goes fire of 18 May 1554.

## Historical source layer

The following source bundle sharpens the already active Goes living-city dossier:

- Brandweer Goes, **Stadsbrand van Goes**: local historical reconstruction placing the salt works outside the walls on the north side of the harbour; gives a harbour-zone inventory of 81 salt pans/keten, identifies one Oostzelke salt pan as the origin, records a strong north-easterly wind, and names Turfkade, the watermill and the Kruisbroeders complex among the destroyed/affected sites.
  - https://www.brandweer-goes.nl/uitrukken/stadsbrand-goes-1554/
- Brandweer Goes, **De brandweer in Goes tot de 19e eeuw**: context for early fire ordinances, compulsory response, ladders/emmer chains and pulling down buildings to create stop-lines; also helps distinguish the 1554 fire from later fire and military-destruction events.
  - https://www.brandweer-goes.nl/historie/1405-1799/
- Zeeuwse Ankers, **Brand!**: describes the fire beginning in a salt pan in the north-east harbour/industrial area, spreading through neighbouring salt works and toward the city under hard wind; roughly one quarter of the building stock is given as lost in the synthesis.
  - https://www.zeeuwseankers.nl/verhaal/brand
- Zeeuwse Ankers, **Zoutzieden**: supports placement of the Goese salt works north of the city and the broader practice of locating such industry outside the walls.
  - https://www.zeeuwseankers.nl/verhaal/zoutzieden

The older `SRC-HIST-GOES-LIVING-CITY-001` and transport-register evidence remain controlling for parcel-level topology and for the mixed post-fire Nieuwstraat/Armenhoek evidence.

## Reconstruction layer: firebrands

For scene blocking only, modern fire-science work may test the physical plausibility of a multi-front fire produced by wind-driven embers/firebrands. It is **not historical evidence for exact Goese roof materials, wind speed, ember range or ignition time**.

Relevant analogues include S. Manzello & S. Suzuki's NIST work on firebrand showers and thatched-roof/decking ignition. The permitted inference is qualitative: once multiple wooden structures burn under strong wind, separate secondary ignitions ahead of the principal flame field are physically plausible.

## Fixed evidence conclusions

1. The salt-work complex relevant to the fire belongs to the **northern harbour-industrial zone**, not a southern salt district.
2. The cited **81 keten** is an inventory figure for the harbour zone, not evidence that all 81 burned.
3. A source-weighted spread corridor can be represented as Oostzelke → neighbouring harbour/salt structures → wind-driven west/south-west secondary ignitions → Turfkade/north-western urban fabric → watermill/Molenwater → Kruisbroeders/western fabric. This is not a minute-by-minute linear ignition sequence.
4. The consulted fire-specific bundle does **not** provide a reliable number of dead or injured. The historical casualty count is therefore `UNKNOWN`; absence of a number is not evidence of zero casualties.
5. Post-fire deeds support **mixed/partial damage** in the old Nieuwstraat/Armenhoek environment, but do not identify the 1542 Nissepat parcel as the burned property.

## Novel-canon boundary

Later author decisions outrank the older PR #4 scene proposal. In current novel canon the 1542 household house is destroyed/uninhabitable, and Tanneken, Jan and the unborn child die in the fire while Claes and Cornelis survive away from the house. These are explicit fictional outcomes grounded in a plausible affected urban environment; they are not historical victim or parcel claims.
