## Supported fiscal definitions

Alva's project of 20–21 March 1569 comprised:

- **Honderdste Penning:** one-time 1% levy on possessions/property.
- **Twintigste Penning:** 5% on sale/transfer of immovable goods, charged to the seller.
- **Tiende Penning:** 10% on sale of movable goods, charged to the seller.
- An additional 10% was proposed on goods for export.
- The Twintigste and Tiende were intended as permanent measures.

## Implementation boundary

The famous tax rates must not be converted into a rule that every merchant in every place simply paid them from 1569 onward.

Alva wrote on **31 October 1569** that agreement on the Honderdste Penning had been reached, while he had modified the demand for the Tiende and Twintigste on merchandise for six years by capitalizing it into an annual lump sum and was still negotiating with the States.

In Zeeland the conflict remained live into early 1572. Dirksen records the final pre-revolt Zeeland States meeting ending on 23 March 1572 with Alva's 24 February plakkaat on the Tiende/Twintigste on the agenda.

## Claes authoring consequence

For 1569–early 1572, the safe economic state is **tax threat / negotiation / administrative preparation / commercial uncertainty**, unless a local source proves actual collection for the specific place, date and transaction.

For Cornelis specifically, the Tiende Penning is highly relevant conceptually because beer and freight are movable commerce, but Cornelis is executed in November 1569 and the novel must not invent a paid 10% beer transaction merely to dramatize the tax.

## Guardrails

- Do not describe the Tiende Penning as a modern VAT without qualification.
- Do not claim universal implementation from the proposal date.
- Keep tax proposal, legal enactment, local collection, buyout and individual payment as separate evidence questions.
