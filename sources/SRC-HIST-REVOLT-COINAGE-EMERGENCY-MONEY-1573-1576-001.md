# SRC-HIST-REVOLT-COINAGE-EMERGENCY-MONEY-1573-1576-001

- **Source ID:** `SRC-HIST-REVOLT-COINAGE-EMERGENCY-MONEY-1573-1576-001`
- **Source type:** numismatic source bundle
- **Scope:** revolt financing by coin countermark and siege emergency money
- **Verification date:** 2026-08-30

## Countermark / `klop` 1573–1574

The hard source basis is the numismatic literature on the `klop van 1573/4`, cross-checked against a modern archaeological-numismatic synthesis.

For Holland, a plakkaat of **7 February 1573** required qualifying coins to be countermarked and accepted at new rates. The difference between old and new rate functioned as an interest-free loan for war finance. A cited example is a rijksdaalder moving from 32 to 36 stuivers after a four-stuiver contribution.

For rebel Zeeland a related countermark regime is supported for 1573–1574, but exact local chronology and implementation are less uniform in the surviving numismatic discussion than for Holland. Treat Zeeland dates and local application with a lower precision unless a local ordinance/coin is identified.

## Emergency money

Rijksmuseum objects and documentation establish the general siege mechanism: prolonged siege could produce a cash shortage, after which a city issued temporary money to pay soldiers and sustain exchange. Materials could include precious metal, copper/tin and, in Leiden, paper.

Leiden has surviving 1574 emergency issues, including a 28-stuiver paper issue dated 10 July 1574. The Rijksmuseum also preserves/illustrates emergency-money examples from multiple besieged Dutch cities.

## Resolver rule

Neither `klop` nor emergency money is a province-wide generic consequence of `war=true`.

Apply only after resolving:

1. date;
2. political control;
3. city/territory;
4. whether a specific countermark or siege issue is supported there.

Royalist Goes in 1573–1574 must therefore not automatically receive the rebel Holland/Zeeland countermark regime.

## Guardrails

- Countermarking changes official circulation value; it does not prove intrinsic silver content changed.
- Do not place Leiden paper emergency money in another city.
- Do not assume all coins in circulation were local issues; mixed coin circulation remains normal.
- A named coin's value must be resolved by year/zone, not by name alone.
