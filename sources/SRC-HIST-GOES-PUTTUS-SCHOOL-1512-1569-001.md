# SRC-HIST-GOES-PUTTUS-SCHOOL-1512-1569-001

## Scope
Historical provenance for Nicolaes van de Put (Puttus) and the pre-Reformation Goese Latin-school tradition relevant to Claes' fictional childhood education.

## Direct / strong historical anchors

### 1512 — Nicolaes van de Put (Puttus) in Goes
P.J. Meertens records **Nicolaes van de Put (Puttus)** as schoolmaster in Goes in 1512. Puttus was a pupil of the humanist Hadrianus Barlandus. Meertens discusses Barlandus' 1512 Aesop school material and accompanying address to `Nicolao Putto`, and concludes from this educational context that Puttus taught Latin/humanist material to the studying youth of Goes and was probably attached to the school as Latin schoolmaster/rector.

Primary accessible synthesis:
- P.J. Meertens, *Letterkundig leven in Zeeland in de zestiende en de eerste helft der zeventiende eeuw* (1943), section **Het onderwijs vóór de reformatie**: https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/meer035lett01_01/meer035lett01_01_0007.php

Meertens' own limitation is important: beyond the 1512 material, very little is known about Puttus and the school.

### 1569 — Latin school explicitly present in Goes
Meertens records that the episcopal school inspection of 1569 found seven schools in Goes, including a Latin school. This proves a pre-Revolt Goese Latin-school environment later in the century, but does not by itself prove institutional continuity from 1512 through Claes' 1550s school years.

Accessible synthesis:
- P.J. Meertens, section on Zeeland's schools / post-Reformation educational history: https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/meer035lett01_01/meer035lett01_01_0029.php

## Specialist histories located

Two specialized histories of the Goese Latin school were located bibliographically:

- R.C.H. Römer, **"De Latijnsche school te Goes"**, *Nehalennia. Jaarboekje voor Zeeuwsche Geschiedenis en Letteren* I (Middelburg, 1849), beginning around p. 79.
- H.W. Fortgens, **"De Latijnse school te Goes"**, *Archief Zeeuwsch Genootschap* (1953), pp. 1–28.

Römer's 1849 *Nehalennia* volume is digitally locatable, and later biographical works cite Römer for seventeenth-century Goese rectors. Fortgens' article is bibliographically confirmed. During the 18 August 2026 audit, the available connector/search layer did not expose the full page text of either article in a form that permitted a reliable page-by-page rector succession extraction.

## Targeted 18 August 2026 audit

Searches were performed for:
- `Nicolaes van de Put` / `Nicolaus van de Put` / `Puttus` / `Nicolao Putto`;
- combinations with Goes, schoolmaster, rector and `ludimagister`;
- later biographical citations to Römer;
- a named successor, departure or death date before 1554.

### Result
No digitally searchable source located in this audit:
- gives Puttus a death date before Claes' school years;
- records his departure from Goes before those years;
- names a successor who demonstrably replaced him before ca. 1550–1554;
- supplies a better named Goese Latin master for Claes' precise period.

This is **negative evidence only**. It does not establish that Puttus held office continuously for four decades.

## Conflicting later synthesis
The *Encyclopedie van Zeeland* states in its broad overview that Goes bought the school in 1414 but that Latin was only taught from 1583. Taken literally, that conflicts with Meertens' evidence for Puttus' Latin/humanist teaching in 1512 and a Latin school in the 1569 inspection. Until Fortgens/Römer or primary municipal evidence resolves the terminology/institutional history, the repository must not use the encyclopedia's 1583 date to erase the earlier documented Latin-teaching tradition, nor infer uninterrupted institutional continuity from the earlier anchors.

## Evidence boundary

**HISTORICALLY SUPPORTED**
- Puttus was schoolmaster in Goes in 1512.
- His educational context is Latin/humanist; Meertens treats him as Latin schoolmaster and probably rector.
- Goes explicitly had a Latin school in the 1569 inspection.

**UNKNOWN**
- Puttus' birth date and death date.
- His exact tenure after 1512.
- Whether he remained continuously in Goes into the 1550s.
- The complete rector succession between 1512 and 1569.
- The exact school building used in Claes' fictional school years.

**NOVEL CANON, NOT HISTORICAL FACT**
- Nicolaes van de Put/Puttus personally teaches the fictional Claes Nissepat in the final pre-fire Goese school years.
