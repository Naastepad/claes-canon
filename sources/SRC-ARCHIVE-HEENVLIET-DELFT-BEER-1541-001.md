# SRC-ARCHIVE-HEENVLIET-DELFT-BEER-1541-001 — Small schuiten and Delft beer route

- **Source ID:** `SRC-ARCHIVE-HEENVLIET-DELFT-BEER-1541-001`
- **Source type:** archival-description narrative based on local Heenvliet records
- **Repository route:** Archives Portal Europe / Streekarchief Voorne-Putten Rozenburg finding-aid material
- **Historical frame:** Heenvliet / Bernisse / Maasland–Delft, around 1540–1541
- **Verification date:** 2026-08-30
- **Use in Claes:** period-contemporary transport-mechanism comparator for Delft beer carried by small schuiten; not a Goes or De Hont-specific route record.

## Evidence

The archival description characterizes the Bernisse around 1540 as used by numerous simple one-sail fishing schuiten. These small vessels were also used for inland navigation. It states that the route from Heenvliet via the Maaslandse sluice to Delft was frequently sailed by Heenvliet skippers because most beer consumed in Heenvliet came from Delft breweries.

The same discussion gives a 1541 incident in which a skipper waited at anchor for the flood tide before entering the harbour, illustrating that even small local movements were tide-dependent and exposed to navigational danger.

## Claes use

This source supports three period-appropriate mechanisms:

1. small schuiten could combine fishing/local work with inland cargo movement;
2. Delft beer already generated recurring waterborne distribution traffic in the 1540s;
3. tide and shallow-water conditions structure movement more realistically than a modern fixed-duration route model.

## Boundary

It does not establish a Heenvliet–Goes route, De Hont's vessel, De Hont's age, or a Delft–Goes travel duration. Use as `C_REGIONAL_MECHANISM`, not as direct local evidence.
