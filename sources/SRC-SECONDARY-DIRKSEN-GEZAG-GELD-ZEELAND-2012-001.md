## Scope used for Claes

This source is used for the institutional and fiscal fragmentation of Zeeland during the first revolt years. It is especially valuable because it distinguishes territories under the `Gemene Zaak` from places that remained under landsheerlijk authority, and because it reconstructs early licent, convoy and impost administration from archival records rather than treating `Zeeland` as one fiscal unit.

## Exact consulted passages

- pp. 68–76: the 1569–1572 conflict around the Tiende Penning and the collection apparatus in Vlissingen.
- pp. 194–210: passport/licent rules and the early Zeeland licent administration.
- p. 200: licentrecht as a permission fee; two early types (war-law and export-ban); convoy money in Zeeland only from 1576.
- pp. 339–343: States and administration in Zeeland under landsheerlijk authority; Goes and Zuid-Beveland remain represented in that side of the divided province.
- p. 430–431: Zeeland licent introduced in 1573 in the rebel area; four-stuiver-per-ton beer levy present from 1 May 1574; levy borne by domestic brewers/production and beer imported from outside Holland and Zeeland.
- p. 555: at the Pacificatie, Zeeland is explicitly described as politically divided: Walcheren/Schouwen/Duiveland/Sommelsdijk in the rebel connection, Zuid-Beveland and Tholen remaining under landsheerlijk authority until reunification by satisfactions.

## Claims supported

1. Zeeland 1572–1576 cannot be queried as a single fiscal or political jurisdiction.
2. Goes/Zuid-Beveland must not automatically inherit rebel-Zeeland taxes, licent rules or monetary interventions merely because they are geographically Zeeuws.
3. Rebel Zeeland has licentrecht from 1573; convoygeld is introduced in Zeeland in 1576.
4. A separate levy of four stuivers per ton of beer is demonstrable in rebel Zeeland from 1 May 1574; its tax point is production/import and it is not a generic retail tax on every biersteker transaction.
5. Licentrecht is permission-based and can arise from war-law/passport control or an export prohibition; exact application depends on route, political control, commodity and date.

## Evidence boundary

Dirksen is a modern synthesis, not a primary act. His exact archival footnotes should be preferred when a scene requires a named collector, exact local tariff, specific ship, passport or individual payment. The source supports a **jurisdiction resolver**; it does not establish that Claes or Cornelis personally paid a specific impost.
