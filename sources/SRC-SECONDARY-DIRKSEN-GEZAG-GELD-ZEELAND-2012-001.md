## Scope used for Claes

This source is used for the institutional and fiscal fragmentation of Zeeland during the first revolt years. It is especially valuable because it distinguishes territories under the `Gemene Zaak` from places that remained under landsheerlijk authority, and because it reconstructs early licent, convoy and impost administration from archival records rather than treating `Zeeland` as one fiscal unit.

It is also useful for the **commercial mechanics of imported beer**: freight, cask loss, remuneration of the steker and the tax valuation of beer are separable cost layers.

- p. 555: at the Pacificatie, Zeeland is explicitly described as politically divided: Walcheren/Schouwen/Duiveland/Sommelsdijk in the rebel connection, Zuid-Beveland and Tholen remaining under landsheerlijk authority until reunification by satisfactions.

## Claims supported

1. Zeeland 1572–1576 cannot be queried as a single fiscal or political jurisdiction.
2. Goes/Zuid-Beveland must not automatically inherit rebel-Zeeland taxes, licent rules or monetary interventions merely because they are geographically Zeeuws.
3. Rebel Zeeland has licentrecht from 1573; convoygeld is introduced in Zeeland in 1576.
4. A separate levy of four stuivers per ton of beer is demonstrable in rebel Zeeland from 1 May 1574; its tax point is production/import and it is not a generic retail tax on every biersteker transaction.
5. Licentrecht is permission-based and can arise from war-law/passport control or an export prohibition; exact application depends on route, political control, commodity and date.
6. Beer sold from Holland into Zeeland could carry distinct **freight**, **cask-loss/recovery** and **steker-remuneration** components; the steker is explicitly interpreted by Dirksen as a Zeeland distribution intermediary rather than the brewer himself.

## Biersteker cost-chain boundary

Dirksen illustrates his interpretation with a hypothetical calculation beginning **`Stel dat`**: if added freight and other costs were 10 stuivers per ton, a 50-stuiver ex-brewery beer could appear as 60 stuivers in Zeeland for impost valuation.

The project must therefore distinguish:

- **SUPPORTED mechanism:** freight + cask loss + remuneration of the steker were recognised additional cost components in beer sold into Zeeland;
- **SUPPORTED role:** biersteker as Zeeland wholesale/distribution intermediary receiving remuneration from the brewer;
- **NOT an observed tariff:** the **10 stuivers per ton** in Dirksen's example is illustrative/hypothetical and may never be entered as a historical freight or biersteker rate.

This mechanism is highly relevant to the professional model for a fictional Goese biersteker, but the 1574 source does not by itself establish a named transaction, exact rate or personal payment for Cornelis.

## Evidence boundary

Dirksen is a modern synthesis, not a primary act. His exact archival footnotes should be preferred when a scene requires a named collector, exact local tariff, specific ship, passport or individual payment. The source supports a **jurisdiction resolver** and a distribution-cost mechanism; it does not establish that Claes or Cornelis personally paid a specific impost or received a particular steker fee.
