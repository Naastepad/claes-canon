# SRC-HIST-1512-PUTTUS-GOES-001

## Type
Historical provenance bundle

## Claim supported
Nicolaes van de Put (Puttus) was a schoolmaster in Goes in 1512. P.J. Meertens, drawing on Hadrianus Barlandus' 1512 Aesop school edition and its accompanying letter to `Nicolao Putto`, concludes that Puttus taught the studying youth of Goes in Latin and was probably attached to the school as rector.

## Sources
- P.J. Meertens, *Letterkundig leven in Zeeland in de zestiende en de eerste helft der zeventiende eeuw* (1943), section "Het onderwijs vóór de reformatie", DBNL: https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/meer035lett01_01/meer035lett01_01_0007.php
- Hadrianus Barlandus, *Pluscule Esopi fabulae* (1512), accompanying letter to `Nicolao Putto`, as cited and interpreted by Meertens.
- R.C.H. Römer, "De Latijnsche school te Goes", *Nehalennia. Jaarboekje voor Zeeuwsche Geschiedenis en Letteren* I (Middelburg, 1849), beginning p. 79. The complete 1849 volume is digitally available through Google Books/Google Play, but the connector used for the August 2026 audit did not expose page text for direct full-text verification.
- H.W. Fortgens, "De Latijnse school te Goes", *Archief Zeeuwsch Genootschap* (1953), pp. 1–28. Bibliographically confirmed; page text was not available through the connector during the August 2026 audit.

## Corroborating school-history anchor
Meertens records that the episcopal school inspection of 1569 found seven schools in Goes, including a Latin school. This demonstrates a pre-Revolt Goese Latin-school environment, but does not by itself prove uninterrupted institutional continuity from 1512 to 1569.

## August 2026 targeted audit
Searches for Puttus/Van de Put after 1512, for an early successor, and for a death/departure date produced no source that places Puttus outside Goes or dead before Claes' 1548–1554 school period. No named Goese rector or Latin master for 1548–1554 was found in the digitally searchable material examined.

This is **negative evidence only**. Failure to find a successor or death record does not prove that Puttus remained in office for four decades.

## Epistemic status
- `FACT`: Puttus was schoolmaster in Goes in 1512.
- `SUPPORTED INFERENCE`: his teaching included Latin/humanist material; Meertens calls him a Latin schoolmaster and probably rector.
- `FACT`: a Latin school is explicitly present in Goes in the 1569 episcopal inspection.
- `UNKNOWN`: Puttus' birth date, death date, departure date, exact tenure and any succession between 1512 and 1569.
- `NOT HISTORICAL FACT`: that Puttus personally taught the fictional Claes in 1551–1554. That is a deliberate story decision recorded separately.

## Reliability / limitations
Meertens is the strongest accessible synthesis and points back to a 1512 printed source. Römer and Fortgens are specialist histories that remain important for a future page-level archival audit. The story may use Puttus as Claes' teacher because the author has explicitly chosen that plausible bridge; prose and metadata must never present the fictional teacher-pupil relationship as documented history.
