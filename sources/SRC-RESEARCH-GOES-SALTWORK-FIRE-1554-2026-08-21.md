Use the sources below according to what they actually support.

- A local archival/public-history source can support a historically bounded claim when its evidence is clear.
- A later Goese or other-city zoutziederij example can be a **structural analogue** without documenting Goes 1554.
- A modern explanatory website can supply vocabulary/research questions without becoming a primary authority.
- A claim pasted into conversation with citations is **not automatically verified merely because citation numbers are present**.

The current historical fire origin, spread, wind and casualty limits remain governed by the existing source claim bundle. Do not overwrite those with a more convenient web summary.

## 4. Historie van Goes — later organizational analogue

URL supplied:
`https://www.historievangoes.nl/18e-eeuw/1740-1746/1740-1746-economische-bedrijvigheid`

This is chronologically much later than 1554, but unusually useful as a **Goese structural analogue** because it distinguishes roles around the salt industry and records a fire incident.

Development extraction to preserve for verification/use as analogue:
- interested owners/investors can be distinguished from daily `keetbaas`/`pannebaas` and workers;
- a later fire can be named by the owner's keet while the immediate careless act is attributed to workers;
- fuel/turf around pans and fire management are explicit later concerns;
- later Goese regulations make visible the permanent physical combination of fuel, heat, pans and combustible work space.

This strongly supports the *plausibility question* of Cornelis as economic interested party without daily operation, but it does **not** prove that the same job titles, liability regime or fuel rules applied unchanged in 1554.

Research lead noted during development: the Goese `panneluidengilde` has records extending back into the sixteenth century; direct inspection of the earliest surviving resolutions would be high-value before final fire prose.

Guardrail:

### Women and girls in salt work

The development conversation highlighted evidence from Alkmaar and broader early-modern research that women and girls could be highly visible in zoutziederij labour, sometimes with distinctive female work hierarchies and clothing in later centuries.

This should **change the research question**, not yet the 1554 fact layer:

Later documented details such as `keetvrouw/keetmeiden`, specific entry ages, breeches or barefoot work must not be back-projected automatically.

## 8. Current authorial process model for Cornelis' keet

Pending finer source control, the Storybible uses this **fictional reconstruction model**:

**import/arrival of rough salt -> specialist refinement in heated pans -> condition/drying -> storage -> onward distribution**

This is preferred because it aligns the material world with Cornelis' stable economic characterization:

**raw material comes from elsewhere -> Cornelis owns/finances/routes -> specialists transform it -> product leaves again.**

Cornelis is thereby an intermediary with real responsibility, not a zoutzieder performing every operation.

## 9. Fire-risk use rule

The combined local/regional/comparative research makes the following authoring principle defensible:

> salt refining is an intrinsically fire-dependent and therefore hazardous industry; catastrophe does not require a bizarre one-off danger invented for the plot.

But scene prose must still distinguish:

- inherent risk;
- ordinary safety practice;
- a particular ignition;
- negligence;
- ownership;
- liability;
- moral blame.

The novel deliberately leaves several of those layers unresolved.
