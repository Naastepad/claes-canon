# SRC-HIST-CATHOLIC-BIBLE-LOWCOUNTRIES-1548-001

Source: *Den gheheelen Bybel, inhoudende het oude ende nieuwe Testament* (Leuven, Bartholomeus van Grave, 1548), digital edition by Nicoline van der Sijs and collaborators.

Supported findings:
- The 1548 Leuven Bible presents itself as a Dutch translation from the old Latin text used in the Roman Church.
- Latin remained the official Catholic ecclesiastical language in liturgy and official documents.
- Nicolaas van Winghe produced the Dutch Catholic translation in the Vulgate tradition under Catholic approval.
- A revision appeared in 1553; Plantin produced an Antwerp reprint with verse division in 1565–1566.
- The same editorial introduction distinguishes the Liesvelt Bible as the first complete Dutch Bible (1526), with a New Testament following Luther; its sixth and last edition appeared in 1542.

Claes use: supports the distinction between Latin liturgical Scripture and Catholic vernacular reading. A Leuven Bible is plausible as a devotional, educational or clerical reading object during Claes' lifetime, but ownership by a specific household remains unproven.
