# De Tachtigjarige Oorlog — Groenveld et al.

## Bibliography
S. Groenveld, H.L.Ph. Leeuwenberg, M.E.H.N. Mout and W.M. Zappey, *De Tachtigjarige Oorlog* (Walburg Pers, 2008 edition represented in the consulted EPUB; ISBN 9789462495661).

## Relevant source-supported findings
- Around 1559 the Netherlands remained institutionally Catholic, while the new episcopal organisation included the bishopric of Middelburg within the archbishopric of Utrecht.
- Calvinist congregation-building in the northern Netherlands was still limited before 1566 and accelerated strongly after Holland and Zeeland broke with central authority from 1572 onward.
- In January 1574 a Habsburg/Spanish fleet was defeated by the rebels near Reimerswaal; the loss of maritime relief contributed to Middelburg's surrender the following month. This is a Delta/waterway-war anchor, not proof of fighting inside the town of Reimerswaal itself.
- Under the post-Pacification consolidation policy, Orange made separate satisfactie agreements with remaining royalist towns. Goes, the last royalist town in Zeeland, reached agreement with Orange in March 1577; according to this synthesis, Catholicism alone was permitted under that agreement.
- During the radicalisation of 1577–1578, local Calvinist groups pushed beyond satisfactie terms. The synthesis states that in Holland and Zeeland Calvinist preaching was introduced under pressure in all towns that had concluded a satisfactie with Orange. For Goes this means the March 1577 Catholic-only settlement should not be projected unchanged through 1578.
- The book presents this transition as politically and confessionally contested: Orange's proposed religious peace in July 1578 sought reciprocal worship rights, but met resistance from both Catholic and Reformed sides.
- Later generalisation: the Reformed public church did not imply instantaneous conversion of the population; confessional affiliation remained plural and unsettled for decades.

## Claes relevance
This source closes part of the previously open Goes/Zeeland chronology. For scenes in Goes:
1. **March 1577:** a satisfactie with Orange can still coexist with explicitly Catholic-only public religion.
2. **1578:** that settlement becomes unstable; Reformed preaching is introduced under local pressure amid wider radicalisation.
3. The transition should be staged as a conflict over public space, preaching, churches, magistracy and legitimacy rather than as an overnight population-wide conversion.

For Reimerswaal, January 1574 provides a strong maritime-war backdrop: the city/area sits within the military geography of the Scheldt and Delta water routes.

## Guardrails
- The source does not give an exact day in 1578 for the introduction of Reformed preaching in Goes.
- It does not establish the exact Goes church building first used for Reformed preaching, the sequence of altar/image removal, or the local identities of activists; those remain local-archive questions.
- Do not infer that all inhabitants changed confession when public worship arrangements changed.
- Do not misattribute this book to Judith Pollmann.
