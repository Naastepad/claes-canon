# SRC-HIST-ERCKER-PROBIERKUNST-1574-001 — Lazarus Ercker and sixteenth-century assay practice

**Type:** primary early-modern metallurgical/assay source  
**Author:** Lazarus Ercker  
**Work:** *Beschreibung allerfürnemisten Mineralischen Ertzt vnnd Berckwercksarten*  
**Edition used as anchor:** Prague, 1574  
**Scope used here:** ores and metals tested in small fire; smelting; separation/testing of gold, silver and other metals.

## Evidence used

The 1574 title and surviving digital records explicitly describe ores being **probirt** and tried **im kleinem fewer**, together with smelting operations and the **separation of gold, silver and other metals**. The work belongs squarely to early-modern *Probierkunst* and provides period support for a professional world in which small-scale fire assays, metal separation and quantitative/observational testing were normal technical practices.

This supports the Claes/Enkhuizen author-side reconstruction that a convincing apparent transmutation could be subjected to a recognisable assay/fire-test culture.

## What this source does not prove

- It does not prove Alexander Seton used Ercker's procedures in Enkhuizen in 1602.
- It does not prove a particular cupellation choreography for the fictional scene.
- It does not prove that lead was converted into gold.
- It does not supply the novel's projectiepoeder composition.
