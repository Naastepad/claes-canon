# SRC-HIST-LEVENDALE-GOES-ANTWERP-1589-001 — Levendale journey from Goes toward Antwerp, 1589

- **Source ID:** `SRC-HIST-LEVENDALE-GOES-ANTWERP-1589-001`
- **Source type:** archival transcription / local edition of Pieter Cornelisz van Levendale's notes with primary RAZE locator
- **Historical actor:** Pieter Cornelisz van Levendale, notary at Goes
- **Primary locator:** `RAZE 2039, fol. 113`, note under 9 October 1589
- **Consulted public transcription:** Chiel Smallegange, *Pieter van Levendale, notaris in troebele tijden*, RAZE 2039 transcription; parallel diary transcription `Levendale als dagboekschrijver`
- **Consulted URLs:** `https://www.chielsmallegange.nl/levendale/raze_2039_boek.pdf`; `https://www.chielsmallegange.nl/levendale/levendale_als_dagboekschrijver.pdf`
- **Verification date:** 2026-08-31
- **Use in Claes:** direct late-sixteenth-century route-scale comparator for Goes / South Beveland toward Antwerp; not a De Hont itinerary and not a civilian freight timetable

## Outbound sequence that is clear in the transcriptions

The note states that on **9 October 1589** Levendale travelled **by wagon to Waarde**. Between about ten and eleven that evening he boarded a **`roeijschute van oorloghe`** in the Geuzen fleet. On the **next day** he was at **Fort Lillo**. On **11 October** he went on past Ordam and other forts under royal control and reached **Antwerp**.

The parallel diary transcription gives the same outbound order and dates. This makes the following sequence usable as a historical comparator:

`Goes -> wagon to Waarde -> late-evening embarkation -> Lillo next day -> Antwerp on 11 October`

## What the source supports

1. A Goes-based traveller could reach the Antwerp corridor through a staged combination of land carriage and water transport.
2. In the wartime conditions of 1589, **Lillo and the fortified Scheldt corridor were operational stages**, not scenery that can be ignored.
3. The movement occupied **multiple calendar days**, and waiting/transfer/political control were structurally part of the journey.
4. The source supports writing route time in **tides, stages and days**, rather than a modern fixed-hour timetable.

## Guardrails

1. Levendale travelled in a **war rowing craft / Geuzen fleet context**, not in Aernouldt de Hont's beer freight vessel.
2. Do not convert the 9–11 October sequence into a universal `Goes -> Antwerp = two days` rule.
3. Do not infer De Hont's exact route, sailing time, vessel, speed or stops from this diary note.
4. The return sequence is transcribed inconsistently across the consulted derivative editions; this source record therefore uses only the clear outbound chronology.
5. For Cornelis' pre-1569 scenes, the evidence supports the route's order of magnitude and tidal/staged logic, not wartime checkpoints that did not yet exist in the same form.
