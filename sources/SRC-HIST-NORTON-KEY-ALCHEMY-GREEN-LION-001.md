# SRC-HIST-NORTON-KEY-ALCHEMY-GREEN-LION-001 — Samuel Norton and the multivalent Green Lion

**Type:** primary/near-primary late-sixteenth-century alchemical manuscript tradition  
**Author:** Samuel Norton  
**Work:** *The Key of Alchemy*  
**Witness used:** transcription from Bodleian Library MS Ashmole 1421; exact composition/copy dating is not fixed here.

## Evidence used

Norton's text explicitly shows that **Green Lion** was not a single stable material label. It distinguishes a philosophical Green Lion from mistaken/vulgar readings, while also acknowledging traditions in which vitriol/green copperas is called or used as a Green Lion in a specific work. Elsewhere the text calls common vitriol/copperas a 'Green Lion of fools' in the context of corrosive-water ingredients.

This supports a precise story guardrail:

- Claes and Dee may operationally call their green vitriol the **Groene Leeuw** in this process;
- the story must not claim that all alchemists universally meant FeSO4 whenever they wrote 'Green Lion'.

## What this source does not prove

- It does not prove Dee used Norton's exact vocabulary in Antwerp in 1564.
- It does not prove a universal Green Lion = vitriol equivalence.
- It does not prove the Claes projectiepoeder route.
