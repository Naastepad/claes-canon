### Goese grain measures

- pp. 249–250: Goese grain measure.
- 1572 Brussels measurement: one Goese `achtendeel` = 70 Middelburg pints / 212 59/128 Brussels uperken, calculated by Boonman at approximately **36.6 litres**.
- `Tresoor` 1590: Goes last recorded as 38 sacks/viertels, each sack = 2 achtendelen; one achtendeel calculated at about **39.605 litres**.
- another transmitted relation gives about **40.1 litres** per achtendeel when the last is treated as 37½ sacks.

**Use rule:** do not freeze a timeless decimal conversion. For sixteenth-century authoring, retain the source/date-sensitive range and original measure name. `1 viertel/Goese sack = 2 achtendelen` is the useful relation; a working volume range is roughly **73–80 litres per sack/viertel** depending on source/date.

### Goese rye transaction, 1595

- p. 252: purchase of rye in Goes in 1595.
- one last had to weigh 4,000 Amsterdam pounds.
- price: **14 schellingen 2 groten Vlaams (170 groten) per sack**.
- price per last: **£26:11:3 Vlaams (6,375 groten)**.
- this corresponds to **37½ Goese sacks per last**.
- primary locator supplied by Boonman: `RAZE 2040, fol. 110, 31 October 1595`.

Derived conversion, not source wording: because 40 groten Vlaams = 20 stuivers, 170 groten = 85 stuivers = 4 gulden 5 stuivers.

### Goese beer measurement

- p. 361 / printed p. 359: late-fifteenth-century Goese beer-measure oath and later references.
- beer stored in Goes had to be measured as it was carried from the quay.
- a poorter could not sell beer before measurement by the sworn `biermeter`.
- poorters are explicitly not exempt from `meetgeld` in 1494; in 1515 everybody inside and outside the city is required to pay meetgeld.

**Evidence boundary:** this is strong local institutional antecedent evidence, but not by itself proof that every wording/tariff remained unchanged in the 1540s–1560s. Use official measurement-before-sale as **SUPPORTED local continuity**, while exact Claes-period ordinance/tariff remains a verification target.

### Goese salt work and labour

- p. 297: salt-production discussion.
- ordinances on refining dated 28 June 1551 and 6 April 1563.
- labour-guild ordinance of **14 September 1546**: carrying/filling/lifting one `honderd grof zout`, performed by seven men, paid **4 groten per man**; `cleynen zoute` per quarter total 5 groten.
- primary locator supplied: `ASG 678, fol. 44 ff., 50 ff. and 53 ff.`.
- Boonman reports approximately 100 salt pans in Goes and a 1569 production estimate, but the printed `ton(?)` unit is itself uncertain and must remain marked as such.
- Boonman states that all saltworks were burned during the 1572 siege and that production resumed at lower capacity.

**Use rule:** the 4-groten payment is **piecework**, not a civilian daily wage.

### Coin/accounting chronology

- p. 428:
  - gold Carolusgulden introduced 1521 at 20 stuivers;
  - silver Carolusgulden introduced 1543 at 20 stuivers / 40 groten Vlaams;
  - silver Carolus remained in use as accounting reference after minting ceased;
  - Philipsdaalder introduced 1557 at 30 stuivers, 35 stuivers in 1559, 45 stuivers in 1579/1580;
  - around 1576 Holland issues the leeuwendaalder at a stated 32-stuiver rate.

Useful accounting relation:

`1 pond Vlaams = 240 groten = 120 stuivers = 6 carolusguldens`

and therefore:

`1 schelling Vlaams = 12 groten = 6 stuivers`  
`1 stuiver = 2 groten Vlaams`.

## Reliability / limitations

Boonman is a modern secondary synthesis drawing heavily on archival metrology. It is unusually valuable for Goes because it gives local measures and archival locators, but conversions into litres are reconstructions and sometimes expose disagreements among historical comparison tables. Preserve the original historical unit and source date whenever exact physical quantity matters.

The same discipline applies to coins: a coin name, accounting unit and physical circulating piece are not automatically identical concepts.
