## Evidence

Footnote 56 records that on **19 February 1585** a `kagheschuyte` from Delft, loaded with butter and cheese, succeeded in reaching Antwerp through Parma's blockade without damage. The underlying contemporary letter was written in Antwerp the following day.

## Claes use

This is a strong late-sixteenth-century corridor/type anchor:

`Delft vessel described as kagheschuyte -> cargo traffic -> Antwerp`

It materially improves the plausibility of using a `kagheschuyte` as a researched fictional type for a Delft-associated shipper operating toward Antwerp.

## Boundaries

- The vessel and skipper in this 1585 episode are not identified as Aernouldt de Hont.
- The cargo was butter and cheese, not beer or salt.
- The blockade episode is exceptional wartime traffic and must not be converted into a normal sailing schedule.
- This establishes a vessel-type/corridor analogue, not De Hont's exact vessel.
