# Review handoff — De Hont route, vessel and contract refinement

**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**PR:** #22  
**Scope:** research refinement only; no merge and no new exact Cornelis-De Hont risk allocation canonized

## Materially new evidence

### 1. Direct Delft/Delfshaven -> Goes corridor in 1572

A published transcription of Delft municipal accounts records vessels lying before Delft and at Delfshaven being requisitioned to carry Barthel Entes and troops from Holland to Goes. Twenty-one vessels were compensated after also being held before Goes.

**Impact:** the Delft/Delfshaven-Goes corridor is now direct Claes-period evidence rather than a reconstruction from 1608-1609 analogues.

**Do not infer:** vessel type, normal freight rate, commercial sailing time or De Hont participation.

### 2. Delft beer moved in heavy schuiten

A 1548 Delft process memorandum, as reported by Verhoeven, describes beer schuiten moving along the Schie toward the Maas. In 1551 Hague beer carriers sought horse towing because their heavy schuiten carrying Delft beer were burdensome to pull manually.

**Impact:** stronger material model for Delft beer logistics and the Delft-Schie-Delfshaven interface.

**Do not infer:** one hull necessarily continued from Delft to Goes.

### 3. Delft kagheschuyte reached Antwerp in 1585

Jongbloet-van Houtte cites a contemporary Andries van der Meulen letter reporting a `kagheschuyte` from Delft, loaded with butter and cheese, reaching Antwerp through Parma's blockade on 19 February 1585.

**Impact:** `kagheschuyte` becomes a strong researched fictional vessel candidate for an Antwerp-facing De Hont scene.

**Do not infer:** that this was De Hont's ship, that it carried beer/salt, or that blockade traffic gives a normal duration.

### 4. Beer contract mechanics have a historical comparator

Asaert summarizes a 1446 Gouda-Antwerp beer-distribution agreement that included commission per sold vat, empty-cask recovery, working capital, termination repayment and compensation for missing casks.

**Impact:** returnable casks, credit/capital and missing-cask liability are period-real contract concerns rather than modern extrapolation.

**Do not infer:** the 1446 rates or conditions were used in 1589 or in Cornelis' fictional agreement.

## Recommended authoring position

- Keep De Hont's exact vessel **OPEN**.
- Default prose to `schipper`, `schuit`, `vrachtschuit` and material description.
- For an Antwerp-specific scene, `kagheschuyte` is now the strongest type candidate, but only as explicit plausible fiction.
- Keep `kromsteven` and `krabschuit` as regional analogues rather than default De Hont identity.
- Model Delft-origin beer in logistical legs: brewery/quay -> beer schuit -> Schie -> Delfshaven/Maas-side interface -> onward regional transport. Do not force transshipment or same-hull continuity.
- Keep De Hont capacity numeric value OPEN.
- Keep exact journey duration OPEN; retain `tides and days, not clock-hours`.
- Keep the actual Horen-De Hont 1589 contractual risk allocation OPEN until the missing `volgen voorwaarden` are recovered.

## Authorial decision still genuinely open

Research can now support a Cornelis-De Hont contract concerned with delivery, freight, reusable casks, credit/accounts and return obligations. It still cannot decide for the author exactly **when loss risk passes from Cornelis to De Hont or to the receiving biersteker**.

That specific split should remain a human story decision if/when a scene requires it.
